# When the work is judged live

A critique, presentation, oral defense or press demonstration leaves little in the files. The evidence is what the instructor observes and how it is graded, so testimony carries more weight here. It still has to be specific.

What to find out, within the slots you already have:

- **What earns the grade** (`graded_on:<anchor>`): is it judged on the quality of the work, on what the student says about it, or on showing up and taking part? A presentation rubric that gives most points to "delivered" is graded on participation; say so in the summary.
- **What the student must say** (`reasoning_evidence`): do they have to explain their choices, answer questions, or defend a decision, and is that part of the grade? Ask openly: "In the critique, what do students have to say about their own work?"
- **Who does the thinking** (`conditions`): does the student lead, or does the instructor or the class point out the problems? Peer critique that the student only listens to is not the student's reasoning.
- **Spoken communication** (stage 5 `habits`): a graded presentation is direct evidence for speaking; record it there when it comes up.

Recording:
- Cite the instructor's message, plus any rubric or prompt in the files.
- Record what students consistently do, as the instructor describes it, not what the best student did once. If the instructor describes one standout case, ask (as your one follow-up) what a typical student does.
- If there is no rubric and the instructor cannot say how it is graded, record that plainly. It is a finding.
