# When the instructor and the materials disagree

Neither side wins automatically. The materials show what the course says it does; the instructor knows what actually happens. The disagreement itself is useful evidence.

1. **Name it once, plainly, in `say`.** Quote the passage and the instructor's words side by side: "The syllabus gives the portfolio 30%, and you said it's mostly a participation grade."
2. **Ask at most one question to resolve it,** and only if the slot depends on it: "Which reflects the course as you teach it now?" Use your one follow-up for that slot. If the slot does not depend on it, ask nothing.
3. **Record the slot with both citations** (the chunk and the instructor's message) and a summary that states both versions until it is resolved, then the resolved version once it is.
4. **The instructor's answer settles practice, not the record.** If they say the rubric is out of date, record what they say students are actually graded on, and note in the summary that the materials say otherwise.

Do not:
- argue or ask the instructor to defend the materials;
- raise catalog disagreements (a stale catalog objective or prerequisite). Those are expected and go to the audit notes through the evidence map, not to the chat;
- raise conflicts between two files; the pre-read already recorded them.
