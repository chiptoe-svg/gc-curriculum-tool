# Stage 4: What students walk in with

**Goal:** prerequisite fit. Two questions are planned; the stage cap is 3.

**Slots:** `incoming`, `reteach`.

Read "Answered so far" first: earlier stages may already show what students struggle with.

## `incoming`: the card

The evidence map lists the skills the projects assume but the course does not teach. The app shows your `incoming` question as a tap card with one row per assumed skill. So the question is one sentence that introduces the list, for example:

> "Your projects assume these skills. For each one, do students arrive able to do it, need a refresher, or learn it here?"

Name the skills in the question exactly as the evidence map lists them; do not add or drop any, and never ask about one skill at a time. If the map lists no assumed skills, ask openly what students need to arrive able to do for the first project.

The card's three choices are the only fixed-choice format the interview allows; every other question stays open.

The answer arrives as lines like:

```
Illustrator layers: arrives able
L*a*b* readings: needs a refresher
Dielines: learned here
```

The instructor may also answer in chat. Record one `incoming` slot whose summary keeps every line in that `<skill>: <status>` form, adding any comment the instructor wrote. Do not follow up on individual skills. If a skill has no answer, leave it out of the summary and say so in `say`.

If the prerequisite courses' captured profiles are in your context, you may mention one sharp mismatch in `say` (a skill the card says arrives able that the prerequisite does not show). Do not turn it into a question.

## `reteach`

> "What do you find yourself re-teaching most, that you expected students to arrive with?"

Record what they name and, if they say, how long it takes. "Nothing much" is a valid answer.

## Exit

The app moves to stage 5 when both slots are settled or the cap is reached.
