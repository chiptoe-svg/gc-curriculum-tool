# Stage 3: When it goes wrong, and the leap

**Goal:** revision, new problems, and the threshold idea. Three questions are planned; the stage cap is 4.

**Slots:** `revision`, `transfer`, `threshold`.

Read "Answered so far" first. Stage 2 answers often settle part of this stage (a rubric line for a reprint, a reflection memo). If an earlier answer fully settles a slot, record it and do not ask.

All three are judgment slots. Open with what the materials show (a revision requirement, a reflection assignment, a resubmission policy, or nothing), then ask openly. Never offer the answer options as a choice: "do they redo it, or move on?" invites a "yes" that proves nothing.

## `revision`

> "When a student's work comes out wrong, say a print that fails, what happens next?"

Listen for: is a redo required, optional or impossible; is there a written or spoken reflection on what went wrong; is the second attempt graded. "There's no time to rerun" is a clear, useful answer; record it.

## `transfer`

> "Where in the course do students meet a problem before you've shown them how to solve it, or take a method they learned to a case that looks different?"

Record where it happens and how much help they have. "It doesn't, really" is a valid answer.

## `threshold`

> "What idea, once it clicks, separates the students who get this course from the ones who don't?"

You want an idea, the understanding behind the work, not a task or a stage of the workflow. If the answer is a task ("they take the file all the way to press"), one follow-up is worth it: "What do they understand at that point that they didn't before?" If the second answer is still a task, record it as given.

## Recording

Record each slot with what the instructor described, citing their message, plus any chunk that shows a revision or reflection requirement. Do not upgrade a description: "they usually fix it" is not "revision is required".

## Exit

The app moves to stage 4 when all three slots are settled or the cap is reached.
