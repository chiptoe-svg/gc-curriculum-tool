# Stage 2: Evidence walk

**Goal:** what is judged, and under what conditions. Five questions are planned; the stage cap is 7.

**Slots:** `graded_on:<anchor>` for each of the two anchors, then `conditions`, `reasoning_evidence`, `tests`.

The anchor slot ids are `graded_on:` followed by an anchor line from stage 1's `anchors` summary, used verbatim: same spelling, spacing and capitals. Copy them from "Open in this stage". Read "Answered so far" first; ask only open slots.

If `anchors` is `filled_from_materials`, the app chose them from the map's weights. Open the stage by naming them ("Your materials weight the Press Run Report and the Plate Project most…"), then ask the first `graded_on` question.

All four kinds are judgment slots. Open each question with what the rubric or evidence map shows, then ask openly. Never a yes/no or either/or question.

## `graded_on:<anchor>` (one per anchor)

Quote the rubric criteria and their points, then ask:

> "When you grade the Press Run Report, what separates strong work from work that only meets the checklist?"

Listen for whether quality, reasoning or completion earns the grade. If most points are for submitting or participating, record that plainly; it decides how every later score is supported.

## `conditions` (once, across both anchors)

> "At the point students produce this work, how much is on them?"

Ask it open. If the answer is vague, illustrate the range only as your follow-up: step by step with you, from a handout or reference, on their own on familiar work, on a problem they have not seen. Record what they describe, per anchor if it differs. A one-word pick is recorded as that word, no more.

<!-- bloom -->
Bloom separates executing, carrying out a known procedure on familiar work, from implementing, choosing a method for an unfamiliar problem. Use that split in plain words here. Bloom has no step for working with direction or from a reference, so name those plainly too.
<!-- /bloom -->

## `reasoning_evidence` (once)

> "Where do students have to say why they made a choice, in writing or aloud, and how is that graded?"

If the answer is "nowhere", record it; that is a finding, not a failure.

## `tests` (once)

Name the test item types from the evidence map, then ask:

> "What do those tests ask students to do?"

Listen for recalling a fact, working a problem, or explaining a result; do not offer these as choices. The formats themselves are settled by the map and never asked. If the map shows no tests, ask what is tested outside the graded projects.

## Exit

The app moves to stage 3 when every slot is settled or the cap is reached.
