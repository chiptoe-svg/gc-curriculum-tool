# Bloom language for faculty-facing wording

Faculty write objectives in the language of Bloom's revised taxonomy. When you write wording a faculty member will read about what students do (an observed outcome, a suggested objective, an interview question, a checklist item), use the taxonomy's named processes where one fits. This is wording only. It never changes how a level is scored; the depth scale and the evidence rule above still decide every level.

## When to use it

Use a process name when it says precisely what students do with the content. Don't force it into other prose, and don't stack several verbs to sound rigorous. One accurate process beats three impressive ones.

## The 19 processes

- **Remember**
  - **recognizing:** picking out the right item when it is shown;
  - **recalling:** producing it without a prompt.
- **Understand**
  - **interpreting:** putting it into another form or their own words;
  - **exemplifying:** giving an example of it;
  - **classifying:** sorting cases into the right category;
  - **summarizing:** stating the gist;
  - **inferring:** drawing a conclusion or predicting what follows;
  - **comparing:** setting two things side by side;
  - **explaining:** stating the cause and effect.
- **Apply**
  - **executing:** carrying out a known procedure on a familiar, routine task;
  - **implementing:** choosing a method and using it on an unfamiliar problem.
- **Analyze**
  - **differentiating:** separating what matters from what doesn't;
  - **organizing:** working out how the parts fit together;
  - **attributing:** identifying the purpose or point of view behind something.
- **Evaluate**
  - **checking:** testing work for errors or consistency;
  - **critiquing:** judging it against criteria or standards.
- **Create**
  - **generating:** proposing alternatives or hypotheses;
  - **planning:** designing a procedure or approach;
  - **producing:** making the product or artifact.

## Fit the process to the evidence

Name the process the student work actually shows. Write it as **process + content + condition**. For example: "students *explain* why ink trap occurs on wet-on-wet runs", or "students *implement* a color-correction method for an unfamiliar substrate". The condition (with guidance, with a checklist, independently, under new constraints) is what tells a reader how far students got. Bloom names the kind of thinking, not how well or how independently it is done, so a verb alone never says that.

## The word "understand"

Bloom's "understand" is a lower category than this framework's Understand, which is reasoning about why. When "understand" would be ambiguous, write the specific process instead: explaining, inferring, comparing. Never put level codes (K2, U4, D3 and the like) in faculty-facing wording. Describe what students do.

## Do wording

For making and performing, use the Apply and Create processes with a condition:
- performing with step-by-step guidance;
- performing with a reference or checklist;
- **executing** independently on familiar work;
- **implementing** or adapting under new conditions;
- **planning** and **producing** original work.

Bloom has no process for working from a reference, and none for a student who guides others. Say those plainly.

## Never inflate

Name the process the evidence shows, never a higher one. If the evidence is unclear, use the plainer process or no process name at all.

- Wrong: the assignment asks students to list the steps of plate-making, and you write "students evaluate plate-making workflows".
- Right: "students recall the steps of plate-making".

A routine check against a standard (a proof against a printed sample, a measurement against a tolerance) is a procedure, not reasoning: describe that routine comparison in plain words ("students match the proof to the printed sample") rather than as comparing, checking or evaluating.

Bloom wording describes the evidence and never stands in for it. A verb in course materials or in a written statement is not evidence of a level.

## Foundational competencies

Agency, attention to detail, resilience, curiosity, and written and spoken communication are outside Bloom's cognitive domain. Describe the behavior plainly ("students check their own proofs against the job ticket before submitting") rather than borrowing a cognitive process name.
