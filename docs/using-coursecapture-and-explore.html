Detailed Guide

Using CourseCapture & Explore

What each step of capturing a course does and why, and how Explore, the course list and the public profile fit around it. For the screen-by-screen steps, use the how-to.

Audience faculty capturing their own courses Time per course 20–45 minutes for a first capture Updated 2026-10-07

§ 1Overview

CourseCapture builds an evidence-backed record of what a course actually develops in students. You check the course materials, an AI interviewer asks you about the course, and the tool drafts a Course Outcome Profile: the course's competencies, each scored for Knowing, Reasoning and Doing at a depth backed by student evidence, plus the five foundational habits (Agency, Attention to Detail, Resilience, Curiosity, Communication), scored for Doing only. You review the draft, adjust what is wrong, and approve it. Approval records a permanent, dated snapshot.

Explore is for thinking about changes to a course once it has been captured. See §9.

Just want the steps?

The step-by-step how-to walks through a capture screen by screen, with screenshots. This guide explains what each step does and why.

§ 2How you reach the tool

The tool runs on the Clemson network. There is no off-campus access; use the campus VPN when you are away.

  • Home page: https://gcworkflow.clemson.edu:8443/ lists every course with its capture status. Anyone on the network can browse it and open a course's read-only public profile.
  • Faculty pages (CourseCapture, Explore, the course list, the program matrix) need a login. GC faculty use the shared faculty username and password, asked once per browser. Faculty who edit only their own course get a personal link from the administrator: clicking it signs them in on that browser for 30 days at a time, and they can change only the courses it lists. Don't share a personal link.
  • Voice input works on the https://…:8443 address. Browsers block the microphone on plain http:// pages, so if the Voice button is refused, check that the address starts with https.

§ 3Before you start

  • The course must be on the course list. If it is missing, use + Add a course on the course list, or ask the administrator. A lecture/lab pair (e.g. GC 3460 + GC 3461) is captured as one bundled course.
  • Have the syllabus ready. A syllabus is required: it is the source of the course's stated objectives. Canvas's syllabus page counts, or upload a PDF or Word file.
  • Gather what students actually do: assignment sheets, rubrics, project briefs, quizzes, lecture slides. The profile can only score what it can see evidence for, so thin materials make a thin record.
  • Allow 20–45 minutes for a first capture. You can stop at any point; the interview is saved and picks up where you left off.

§ 4Step 1 · Check your materials

The first screen shows everything the interviewer will read, in three boxes. Open each one to see what is inside and add what is missing.

  • Canvas — assignments, quizzes, pages, modules, the Canvas syllabus and Canvas files. Import from Canvas / Reimport needs a Canvas API token. Scan linked docs follows Google Docs, Drive PDFs and YouTube links inside your Canvas content and pulls them in.
  • Syllabus & course info — the syllabus (required) and, for courses that have one, the course's row on the GC course sheet. Upload a syllabus here if Canvas doesn't have one.
  • Other materials — anything else: slides, rubrics, handouts, exemplars. PDF, Word, PowerPoint and Excel files are read; scanned PDFs are read with OCR.

Pick your name under I'm the instructor so the capture is attributed to you, then click Continue →. The step won't continue without a syllabus.

Privacy

Before anything is stored, the tool removes email addresses, Social Security numbers and CU ID numbers from the text it reads (instructor contact details in a syllabus are kept). The original files are not changed. The AI service is covered by Clemson's education contract.

§ 5Step 2 · Choose how closely to read each file

Reading every file in full is slow and not always useful, so each file gets a level. The tool makes a first guess; your job is to check it.

LevelWhat the tool doesUse it for
High: read in fullEvery passage is stored and can be quoted as evidence; pictures are described.The syllabus and anything graded: assignments, rubrics, quizzes, exams.
Middle: summarized slide by slideEach slide or section becomes a short note of what it teaches; title-only and image-only slides are dropped.Lecture slides, class pages.
Background: one summaryThe whole file becomes one paragraph. The tool knows what it covers but can't quote it.Readings, references.

Why it matters: a score above the basics needs evidence that students actually did the work, so anything graded must be High or it can't count as evidence. Use ▲ ▼ to move a file, ignore to leave it out, delete to remove it. Moving a file that was already read re-reads it at the new level.

Click Read files & continue. The estimate beside the button counts only files not yet read. When reading finishes you go on to the interview automatically; the step stops only if a file couldn't be read, so you can go back and replace it or continue without it.

§ 6Step 3 · The interview

Choose Build on prior capture (the interviewer starts from the last approved snapshot) or Fresh capture (it starts from the materials only), then Start the interview.

What the interviewer is doing

It has read your materials and can search them during the conversation. It also knows the profiles of the courses that feed into yours and the courses that build on it, from the Clemson catalog's prerequisites, so it can ask about hand-offs ("GC 4060 expects students to arrive able to … — does your course prepare them for that?"). Those neighboring courses are context for its questions, never evidence for your scores. It checks:

  • what students actually produce and how it is graded, as opposed to what the syllabus aspires to;
  • how deeply students know, reason about and do each thing;
  • whether the stated objectives match the evidence;
  • where students struggle, fail and revise.

Answering

Each turn ends with a highlighted Question for you. Type a reply (Enter sends, Shift+Enter adds a line) or click Voice to speak it; the first recording can take a few seconds to start. Specific answers ("students submit a press-ready PDF and I grade it against this preflight checklist") produce a much stronger profile than general ones. If a document the interviewer can't open (a video, an external tool) matters, describe what is in it.

The readiness bar

Below the reply box, the readiness bar shows how much the interviewer thinks it has covered, with the topics covered so far and those still being probed. It is a guide, not a gate.

Stopping and resuming

Your answers are saved after each turn. Open the course again and you return to the conversation. Where we left off summarizes earlier sessions.

Finishing

Click I'm done — give me one last question. The interviewer looks back over the conversation and asks the single most important question still missing. Answer it, then click Generate Profile.

§ 7Step 4 · Generate, check outcomes, review and approve

Generating

Generating takes about two minutes. One pass reads the whole interview and your materials, identifies the course's competencies (typically 5–15) and scores each one. Any score above the basics must carry an evidence excerpt that traces back to a real passage in your materials or a turn in the interview; results that don't check out are rejected and redone. The result is a draft. Nothing is recorded yet.

Checking the outcomes

Two short screens come first: Apparent outcomes (what students can do by the end, as the interview read it) and Incoming skills (what the course assumes students arrive with). Each has three choices:

  • ✓ Looks good — proceed;
  • Add or change something — describe the change and the tool suggests the edit for you to accept or discard;
  • ← Back to the interview — if it missed something important.

Reviewing the profile

The review page starts with Does this capture your course?, an overview of what the interview found. Below it, the competencies are listed in course order. A second AI review runs automatically after generating and checks each score against the evidence; the cards it, or the interviewer, was least sure about are highlighted as worth a look, each with the reason.

Each highlighted card needs a decision:

  • ✓ Looks right if it is correct;
  • Needs adjusting to change a score: click Change beside Knowing, Reasoning or Doing, pick the description that fits, and Save changes. Raising a score asks you for the evidence, which is stored as the reason.

Tags on each card say where a reading came from: From your interview, From course materials or AI's inference. Click a citation to see the passage. Flag this reading records a disagreement that stays visible until someone resolves it. Save draft keeps your changes without approving.

If the overview gets the course badly wrong (not just a score or two), use ← Back to the interview, tell the interviewer what it missed, and generate again.

Approving

When every highlighted card is done, Approve the profile unlocks at the bottom of the page (it reads Approve update for a course that already has a snapshot). Add an optional caption and note. Approval records a permanent, dated snapshot; the green Captured card confirms it. The course's public profile, the program coverage matrix and the curriculum wiki update from it automatically.

§ 8Snapshots and re-capturing

Every approval adds a dated snapshot; nothing is overwritten. The working draft stays editable after approval, and later changes are recorded with Approve update as a new snapshot.

The Snapshots panel (under Setup & history on the interview step, or the N versions link on the course list) lists them. For each one:

  • view opens a read-only summary;
  • use as draft replaces the current working draft with that snapshot's profile (the snapshots themselves are untouched);
  • retire hides a snapshot that no longer describes the course; restore brings it back.

When to re-capture

Re-capture after a substantive change: new or redesigned assignments, a change of instructor, a shift in what the course emphasizes. Open the course, choose Build on prior capture, and the interviewer starts from the last snapshot and asks what has changed.

§ 9Explore · thinking through a change

Explore (/explore/<course-code>, the Explore Changes link on the course list) is a conversation about a course that has been captured. It is anchored to the course but can draw on the whole program record. Starter questions include:

  • What does this course set up for downstream courses?
  • Which career targets does this course support best?
  • What concepts are anchored in this course?
  • How does this compare to its prerequisites?

Type or use Voice, then Send. Answers cite the curriculum wiki pages they draw on.

Trying a change

Once the course has been captured, describe a change ("What if the capstone required a written rationale for every design decision?") and Explore predicts its effect against the course's latest snapshot: which competencies would move, and the ripple on downstream courses and career fit. Each prediction appears as a scenario card with three buttons:

  • Save names the scenario so you can come back to it;
  • Compare asks Explore to compare your saved scenarios;
  • Adopt makes the scenario the course's next planned version: it replaces the current working draft with the scenario's intended targets and revised design. Measured scores and snapshots are not changed.
After adopting

The capture page doesn't yet mark a draft as adopted from a scenario. If you adopt one, you'll see the changed targets in the draft but no note explaining where they came from. Adopt only when you mean to plan the next version from it.

§ 10The course list and prerequisites

The course list (/courses)

Courses are grouped by level, with counts by status at the top. Each row shows:

  • a data badge: Measured (captured from evidence), Syllabus or No data;
  • a status: Captured, Reviewed, AI drafted, In audit or Not started, and the last capture date;
  • the main action: Capture Course if not started, Edit Course otherwise;
  • View Course (the public profile), Explore Changes, N versions (the Snapshots panel) and Prereqs.

+ Add a course adds one course (code, title, level, track, catalog URL). Preload courses adds many at once, one per line.

Prerequisites

The tool takes prerequisites from the Clemson catalog, falling back to the GC course sheet only for a course the catalog doesn't list. The interviewer, the profile and the wiki all use this.

The Prereqs page for a course (/courses/<code>) lets you record skill-level prerequisite links: which competency, at what Knowing / Reasoning / Doing depth, a course needs from each prerequisite. Seed from syllabus prerequisites drafts them with AI; you confirm, edit or delete each one. The gap view below compares what each prerequisite delivers (from its captured profile) with what this course needs, using confirmed links only: Met, Gap or No data.

Note

The text headed "Catalog prerequisites" on that page is still the GC course sheet's wording, not the Clemson catalog list used everywhere else.

§ 11The public profile

Every captured course has a public page at /view/<course-code> (View Course on the course list). Anyone on the Clemson network can open it without logging in, which makes it the link to share with a chair, a colleague or an accreditation reviewer. It shows the latest snapshot's outcomes and what the course develops, with a Markdown download and a print button. Courses not yet captured show their catalog description instead.

It does not show the interview transcript, the review notes or individual scoring evidence. Those stay on the faculty pages. Student emails and ID numbers never appear: they are removed before anything is stored.

The curriculum wiki (/wiki) is also public and is rebuilt from each new snapshot.

§ 12Tips

  • Put graded work in High. It is the evidence the scores rest on.
  • Be concrete. "Students preflight their own files against a checklist and fix every error before submission" beats "students learn preflight."
  • Don't pad the habits. A foundational habit (Agency, Attention to Detail, Resilience, Curiosity, Communication) at Doing "not yet" is a real finding: it shows where in the program the habit is and isn't developed.
  • Describe what the tool can't open. Videos on Vimeo, Panopto, Kaltura or Canvas Studio, and external tools, are noted but not read. Tell the interviewer what is in them.
  • Use Voice for long explanations and typing for short corrections.
  • If a page acts strangely right after the tool was updated, reload it (Shift-Reload).

§ 13What these tools don't do

  • Not an evaluation of any instructor or student. Scores describe what the course's work requires and evidences.
  • Not automatic redesign. Explore predicts and suggests; faculty decide, and a re-capture records the change.
  • Not a replacement for your judgment. The AI drafts; you confirm. Every score is editable.
  • No snapshot comparison yet. You can read snapshots one at a time, but there is no side-by-side view of what changed.

For what Knowing, Reasoning and Doing depth mean and what counts as evidence, see the KUD+ Background document. For where the program is heading, see the Vision document.
