# GC career paths: the five targets as a value chain

GC organizes its curriculum around five career targets that together cover the value chain of the work GC graduates do. They are a working frame, open to revision. This page condenses section 3 of *AI and GC Careers*. Back to the [[ai-careers-brief|AI and GC careers brief]].

## What the evidence says

### The chain

In value-chain order, with each target's standing number:

- **Decides — 3. [[brand-strategy|Brand Strategy & Experience]].** The decision-maker at a brand or agency: directs creative work, channels, markets and technology in a business-driven strategy role.
- **Designs — 4. [[creative-generalist|Purposeful Design & Creative Generalist]].** A designer with depth in one specialty (graphic, UX/UI or package design) who understands the business and technology consequences of their decisions, works fluidly across media, and builds AI tools for their own use.
- **Makes — 1. [[production-operations|Production & Operations]].** Makes it real: process control, quality, estimating, planning and scheduling, process engineering, equipment justification and managing people, at printers and converters, brand production and procurement, or agency production.
- **Supplies — 2. [[account-management|Sales Solutions & Account Management]].** The vendor-side solution seller: sales, account management, technical sales and customer service. The skills are technical and fiscal: understanding the process, building the ROI case, making the numbers work.
- **Enables — 5. [[ai-workflow|Creative Technology & Systems]].** Builds and runs the tools, systems and processes the other four use: workflow, creative operations, asset management, color management, IT support, systems analysis, platforms and integration. AI is central: implementing, benchmarking and evaluating it, not just using it.

The thread GC adds across all five is an understanding of downstream consequences: what happens to the work after it leaves each person's hands. Targets 4 and 5 differ by who the tool is for: a creative generalist builds tools for their own use, a creative technologist builds tools and systems other people use (AI and GC careers, §3).

Joined to OpenAI's framework categories, recent GC first jobs differ sharply from US employment: 64.0% are in occupations labeled "grows with AI" (US 11.9%), 17.1% "higher automation risk" (US 17.7%), 15.6% "will reorganize" (US 23.2%) and 3.3% "less immediate change" (US 47.3%). These are shares of the 642 first jobs (97.0% of 662) whose occupation code OpenAI's file scores, same data stamp. The categories are a map built on model judgments in vendor research, not forecasts; the file labels graphic designers "grows with AI" while BLS projects decline (AI and GC careers, §2).

### Each path and AI

- **Brand Strategy & Experience: growing, squeezed entry.** Marketing specialists and managers are projected to grow about 7% (2025–35). Marketing is among the most AI-exposed occupations, with pressure on entry roles such as report production; strategy itself looks augmented rather than replaced, on limited evidence. About 20% of first jobs, three-quarters of them entry marketing roles. Strategy is a destination: 98 graduates later reached senior brand or marketing titles (matched on title words), 55 of whom started outside this target. Evidence: moderate to strong on growth, limited on the entry squeeze.
- **Purposeful Design & Creative Generalist: under pressure.** Graphic designers are projected to decline (−1.7%), with AI named as a factor; design graduates' relative pay fell; freelance graphic design job posts fell 18.5% relative to manual-intensive jobs after image generators launched (a working paper). Digital and interface design is projected to grow (+6.0%). The largest target, about 28% of first jobs; graphic designers are about half of it, UX/UI/web under 3% of all first jobs. No sign yet of a decline in GC graduates' graphic-design first jobs, though the numbers are too small to read as a trend. About a quarter of designers later broaden into art direction, UX/web or video. Evidence: strong that graphic design is under pressure; the case for the cross-media designer who directs AI is reasoned, not measured.
- **Production & Operations: reshaping.** Routine prepress (−15.3%) and press work (−8.7%) are projected to decline; the industry reports a skills shortage, and packaging is forecast to grow. Printers report using AI for estimating, scheduling and preflight; process engineering, equipment justification and managing people are not named AI uses. About 20% of first jobs, about half of them project management and line prepress. About a fifth of line-prepress starters later move into the systems or sales targets. Evidence: strong on the decline of routine work; moderate on the shortage and AI uses (trade surveys and vendors).
- **Sales Solutions & Account Management: splitting.** Technical sales pays about twice the median and holds steady (BLS +1.2%); customer service, the on-ramp, is projected to shrink (−5.3%). The conversational and clerical layer is the most exposed; sales shows among the lowest estimated AI time savings (model estimates, vendor research), though BLS rates technical sales "Very high" for AI exposure. About 20% of first jobs; about a quarter of customer-service starters later move into account or sales roles. Evidence: the thinnest of the five, with no independent study of AI and print or packaging sales.
- **Creative Technology & Systems: emerging.** BLS projects growth for systems analysts (+7.9%), management analysts (+10.1%) and software developers (+10.2%), all rated very highly AI-exposed. IT support is projected to shrink (−3.5%) and is better treated as a bridge than a destination. The few entry postings pay about $52k–$90k; senior AI creative-technologist roles $120k–$226k after 4–10 years (an internal brief compiling postings and pay surveys). About 4% of first jobs, mostly IT support, analyst and platform roles; creative operations and color management are 6 of the 26. Evidence: strongest qualitative case, weakest data.

(All of the above: AI and GC careers, §2–§3.)

### Why the newest path is hard to count

No federal occupation code exists for workflow and creative-technologist roles. Thirty-four GC graduates have held 42 positions with workflow, automation or technologist in the title, scattered across seven unrelated occupation groups (AI and GC careers, §2); only 4 were first jobs (KUD+ Background, §16).

## Our reading

- **Careers move along the chain,** from design, customer service and line prepress toward strategy, accounts and systems. Strategy and the workflow side of target 5 are mostly reached later, not as first jobs.
- **Exposure differs by path, and so does the evidence.** In every link, executing variants is squeezed while specifying, governing and integrating grows (AI and GC careers, §2). The squeeze is best evidenced for graphic design and routine prepress, least for sales.
- **Target 5 is the priority and the least certain.** It is the highest priority and the least covered; growth of the category is unproven, and with about two dozen graduates per horizon its shares are unstable (Competency catalog, §11).
- **Hold the alumni figures lightly.** One coder, pending a second-coder check; the framework categories are model labels, not forecasts.
