# Curriculum implications: what the evidence suggests GC could do

This page sets out what the program's coverage records and research show about the curriculum today, then the moves the papers recommend. Everything under "Our reading" is **Proposed — for faculty discussion**. The curriculum tool informs the committee; it does not make or approve changes (Executive brief). Back to the [[ai-careers-brief|AI and GC careers brief]].

## What the evidence says

### The curriculum today

The GC core is 16 courses across four levels, from orientation and digital graphics, through imaging, inks and substrates, to packaging, commercial printing, planning and control, two seminars and two internships. A further 14 specialty courses, including the brand sequence, sat outside the program's career-target matrix when the paper was written (AI and GC careers, §4).

The curriculum tool records two kinds of coverage that must not be merged. **Intended** coverage is what catalog text claims. **Captured** coverage is what faculty interviews show students actually do, scored 0–5 on Know, Understand and Do, with any score above zero requiring evidence of what students produced (AI and GC careers, §4; Executive brief).

The record as read on 5 October 2026 (AI and GC careers, §4):

| Career target | Courses claiming hands-on depth (intended) | Hands-on depth in captured evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production & Operations | 15 | GC 3460, GC 4440 |
| Sales Solutions & Account Management | 16 | GC 3460, GC 4440 |
| Brand Strategy & Experience | 12 | GC 3460, GC 4440 |
| Purposeful Design & Creative Generalist | 17 | GC 3460, GC 4440 |
| Creative Technology & Systems | 1 | GC 4440 only |

"Hands-on depth" means a Do score of 3 or more. The captured column came from June 2026 interviews covering five courses, scored with AI assistance from faculty interviews, not student assessment data. Target 5 was scored under its earlier, narrower "AI Workflow / Orchestrator" definition.

What the paper draws from this:

- On paper the curriculum covers four of the five targets broadly. Creative Technology & Systems is the clear gap in both sources: one course claims hands-on depth (GC 3790, Brand Agency Leadership Practicum) and one shows it in captured evidence (GC 4440, Current Developments & Trends).
- In GC 3460 (Ink & Substrates), students know and understand workflow concepts well but rarely practice them, a "theory without craft" pattern.
- AI appears in the learning objectives of one course (GC 3780, Brand Agency Practicum) and in the projects or descriptions of two more (GC 1050 and GC 3790).
- The picture is partial: coverage scoring had not run since mid-June, twelve courses had no captured evidence, and the two internships had no recorded objectives. The direction is clear; the counts will change when scoring is rerun.

### What has changed since, and what is not built

- The executive brief (updated 9 October 2026) reports 19 of the 59 catalog courses captured. The paper's table reflects only the five courses scored in June; as of 5 October, coverage scoring had not run since mid-June. In October, courses affected by two competency merges were re-scored against the merged descriptions (23 snapshots) (Executive brief; AI and GC careers, §4; Competency catalog, §04).
- In October 2026 competencies became one shared catalog that no single career owns; every program-visible GC course now counts toward each career that claims a competency, including courses off the career paths. The four new business-acumen competencies are not yet scored (Competency catalog, §03, §06, §09).
- Career requirements carry no weights or required depths yet. Linking the catalog to O*NET and to the European skills classification ESCO, then weights, then a readiness view showing shortfalls, are planned, not live (Competency catalog, §10).
- An employer input form is built, but no employer has completed one, so the comparison of program coverage against employer demand has not been run (Executive brief).
- The Graduate Outcome Criterion-Relevance Study has collected its data; its alignment analysis waits on a signed pre-registration and an independent second-coder check. It tests whether the curriculum emphasizes what graduates' destinations demand, not student attainment or causation (Graduate outcome study).

### What the framework says about AI

The KUD+ Background concludes that the AI evidence calls for refinements of emphasis, not new machinery (KUD+ Background, §16):

- consider giving Understand more weight as a readiness signal in AI-augmented destinations, provisional until the graduate-outcome study reports;
- harden the evidence rule against AI-produced artifacts by leaning on the upper depth levels: reasoning through new cases, adapting to new conditions, critiquing and extending work;
- keep career destinations defined from evidence rather than from federal occupation codes.

The problem-solving deep dive names what would change a curriculum decision: missing upper-depth opportunities for a target; high-performing courses with no productive-failure design ("unproductive success"); no structured reflection in capstones or upper-division courses; and a sequence that expects mastery before it is built (Problem-solving deep dive, one-page version).

## Our reading

**Proposed — for faculty discussion.** Recommendations from the program's papers and research documents, offered as things to consider. None has been decided.

1. **Re-weighting rather than replacing.** The AI evidence changes the weights, not the framework (AI and GC careers, §5).
2. **Treating judgment as a skill to build and assess.** Courses could give students more practice evaluating, selecting and directing work, including AI output. Because the case is a hypothesis, the Understand weighting could stay provisional until the outcome study reports (AI and GC careers, §5; KUD+ Background, §16).
3. **Asking for evidence AI can't fake.** Assessment could lean on reasoning through new cases, adapting to changed conditions, and critiquing and extending work, rather than a single finished artifact (AI and GC careers, §5).
4. **Building the Creative Technology & Systems target.** It is the highest priority and the least covered. An industry panel could define the role's tasks, entry path and what counts as evidence of readiness (AI and GC careers, §5–§6).
5. **Teaching the content supply chain, not just content:** design systems, variable-data templates and scripting, generative pipelines, digital asset management and brand governance (AI and GC careers, §5).
6. **Leaning into packaging versioning and compliance:** artwork-management platforms, regulatory labeling, 2D barcode standards, color across flexo and digital, and the cost of short digital runs (AI and GC careers, §5).
7. **Pairing marketing with measurement.** Testing and attribution are the work that grows when variants get cheap (AI and GC careers, §5).
8. **Building the missing first rung into the program.** Internships, co-ops and capstones could leave students with evidence of systems and judgment work: a template system, an automated versioning workflow, a compliance-checked line extension (AI and GC careers, §5).
9. **Describing careers from evidence, not occupation codes,** using postings, alumni outcomes and industry input (AI and GC careers, §5).

**Before acting on counts,** coverage scoring would need a full rerun on the current captures, since the numbers above predate most of them. Sales Solutions & Account Management also needs its own research: no independent study of AI and print or packaging sales exists (AI and GC careers, §3, §6).

**How sure we are.** The direction is consistent across the papers and framework documents, but the labor-market evidence is mostly working papers and vendor research, the coverage picture is partial, and the alumni data are descriptive and single-coder.
