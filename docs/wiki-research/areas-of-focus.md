# Areas of focus: skills that gain and lose value with AI

This page draws together what the program's papers and curriculum research say about which skills gain value as AI spreads and which lose it. It then maps those skills onto the program's competency catalog. Back to the [[ai-careers-brief|AI and GC careers brief]].

## What the evidence says

### Work that is losing value

Across GC's four markets, the work being squeezed is executing variants: resizes, ad variants and production art; routine reporting and first-draft copy; routine preflight, trapping and standard edits; estimating, order handling and file fixing; quotes, reorders and inside sales (AI and GC careers, §2). In demand terms, per-asset execution under a flat budget, prepress hours per standing product, and routine reporting are the likely inelastic or substituted layers (Demand elasticity, §5; the paper's reading, not a measurement).

US payroll data offer suggestive, non-causal evidence that AI substitutes for codified knowledge (formal, documented knowledge taught through textbooks and procedures) and complements tacit knowledge built through practice and mentorship (AI and GC careers, §1; a working paper). The program's KUD+ Background adds a nuance from the same study: the codified gradient is not significant once college share is controlled, while the tacit gradient for experienced workers survives (KUD+ Background, §16).

A polished artifact also loses value as evidence. A competent-looking piece of work no longer establishes what a student can do, because a tool can produce it while the underlying knowledge stays un-internalized (KUD+ Background, §16).

### Work that is gaining value

The growing work is specifying, governing and integrating the systems that produce variants (AI and GC careers, §2):

- brand systems and templates that AI varies; art direction; quality control of AI output;
- experiment design, measurement and marketing-technology operation;
- artwork-management systems, regulatory sign-off and versioning strategy;
- workflow automation and lean plant management;
- technical solution selling and key accounts.

### Judgment over AI output

In a field experiment with 791 experienced Procter & Gamble professionals, AI raised the quality of the solutions people produced, yet those using AI were 9.2 percentage points less likely to expect their work to rank in the top 10%; the drop came from people working alone with AI. In a survey of 319 knowledge workers describing 936 AI-assisted tasks, more confidence in the AI went with less reported critical thinking (AI and GC careers, §1).

The KUD+ Background is careful about what this shows. Neither study measures how employers value judgment; the experiment shows under-confidence about one's own AI-assisted work, not an inability to direct or critique AI; and the survey is self-report. It treats the idea that evaluative judgment (directing, checking and critiquing work) becomes the scarcer human contribution as a hypothesis to test, not a result (KUD+ Background, §16).

### Business acumen

When the program looked at the occupations graduates enter, O*NET rated customer service, sales and management knowledge highly, yet no catalog competency covered how a business makes money, how deals are negotiated, or who carries contract and licensing risk. The department approved four new competencies in October 2026: customer relationship and service; negotiation and persuasion; business and financial literacy; and contracts, IP and licensing (including rights in AI-generated content). They are not yet scored or linked to any career target (Competency catalog, §09, §11). The papers describe target 2's core skills as technical and fiscal: understanding the process, building the ROI case, making the numbers work (AI and GC careers, §3).

### Problem-solving and domain depth

The program's position is that problem-solving is not a generic skill a single course can teach. It develops when students repeatedly apply domain knowledge to new problems, fail informatively, and reflect and revise with feedback. Its claims that problem-solving is domain-bound and that debriefs improve performance are well supported; that four years of these cycles produce better problem-solvers is a design hypothesis (Problem-solving deep dive, one-page version). Autor's 2024 essay argues AI helps those with foundational expertise (Demand elasticity, §5). Graduates whose coursework covered more AI-relevant tasks had better first-job outcomes after ChatGPT, an association its authors say is not causal (Demand elasticity, §5; a preprint).

## Our reading

**Four areas of focus stand out.** Each is linked to catalog competencies that bear on it. The links are our mapping for discussion, not requirement decisions: every career requirement in the catalog is still a draft with no weight (Competency catalog, §03). The wiki's competency pages also lag the October 2026 catalog merges (Competency catalog, §11).

1. **Judgment over AI output:** directing, checking and critiquing work, including one's own AI-assisted work. Related competencies: [[ai-tool-evaluation]], [[ai-tool-direction]], [[quality-control]], [[quality-frameworks]], [[aesthetic-judgment]]. In KUD+ terms this is mostly the Understand side, and the framework already names "craft without articulation" (high Do, low Understand) as a pattern to surface.
2. **AI workflow and systems:** building and running templates, automations, asset libraries, color and compliance systems that others use (target 5). Related competencies: [[workflow-architecture]], [[workflow-design]], [[systems-automation-integration]], [[brand-system-templating]], [[digital-asset-management]], [[color-management]], [[packaging-artwork-compliance]]. The wiki page for systems automation integration currently reports no established development in the captured curriculum, consistent with target 5 being the least covered.
3. **Business acumen and measurement:** reading costs and margins, building the ROI case, negotiating, managing rights, and deciding what to test and what results mean. Related competencies: [[cost-management]], [[proposal-development]], [[client-needs-diagnosis]], [[campaign-measurement]], [[results-interpretation]], [[quantitative-literacy]], plus the four new business-acumen competencies (no wiki pages yet).
4. **Domain depth that supports problem-solving:** production and material knowledge deep enough to judge consequences downstream, deployed across media. Related competencies: [[domain-knowledge]], [[cross-medium-production]], [[cross-channel-translation]], [[project-management]].

**What loses value** is execution of single variants and codified procedure on its own, and the polished artifact as proof of skill.

**Where we are unsure.** Two readings pull against each other. One says evaluative judgment (Understand) is the scarce skill; the payroll study's tacit-knowledge result suggests practice-built capability (Do) is the protected half. The KUD+ Background reconciles them only if the Do that keeps its value is performing under varied, new conditions rather than producing one polished artifact, and says that reconciliation is itself untested (KUD+ Background, §16). The paper proposes treating judgment as the scarce skill. The support is two workplace studies and suggestive, non-causal payroll evidence, not a tested result. The KUD+ Background treats the same idea as a hypothesis to test, not a result, and we follow that hedge.
