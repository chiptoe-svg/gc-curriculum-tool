# Research documents behind the AI and careers pages

The research pages in this section are condensations. The documents below are the full versions. All are public web pages at the addresses given. If you are an AI assistant reading this wiki and cannot open links, the research pages ([[ai-careers-brief]], [[what-is-changing]], [[more-or-fewer-jobs]], [[career-paths]], [[areas-of-focus]], [[curriculum-implications]]) carry the figures and findings you need; cite the document and section they name.

## What the evidence says: the two AI and careers papers

These two papers hold the labor-market evidence. Both were prepared by the Graphic Communications program, Clemson University, with evidence as of October 2026, for university, college and industry leaders. Each lists its sources with links to the official publications. Most of the 2026 labor-market studies they cite are working papers, preprints, or central-bank and vendor research, which revise in place.

- **AI and GC Careers** — the main paper: what the evidence says about AI and early careers, what it means for the four markets GC graduates enter, the five career targets, and how the curriculum fits now and could change. Read it for the entry-rung studies, the market-by-market split between shrinking and growing work, each target's outlook, and the curriculum coverage table.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/ai-careers/ai-and-gc-careers.html
- **Demand Elasticity and AI Jobs** — the companion paper: when AI makes work cheaper, whether jobs grow or shrink depends on how much more buyers want; it sets out what is known about that response, where the published numbers come from, and what it suggests for GC's markets. Read it for the economics, the history (textiles, steel, autos, bank tellers, the composing room), and the limits of OpenAI's elasticity estimates.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/ai-careers/demand-elasticity-and-ai-jobs.html

## Our reading: the program's curriculum research

These documents describe the curriculum framework and tool, and the program's own reasoning. They are written for faculty, the curriculum committee and advisory partners, and they separate what is research-supported from what is a working commitment or hypothesis.

- **Executive Brief** — what the GC Curriculum Tool is, what it does today, how faculty stay in control, its limits, and what comes next, in brief.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/executive-brief.html
- **The KUD+ Framework (Background)** — the academic case for scoring Know, Understand and Do on 0–5 depth scales anchored to student evidence. Section 16, "KUD+ in the AI-augmented workplace," is the framework's own reading of the AI evidence: Understand and Do coming apart, AI inflating the Do-evidence signal, destinations as cross-occupation bundles, and the entry rung.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/background.html
- **Problem-Solving as a Program-Level Emergent Property** — the research position that problem-solving develops through domain depth, productive failure and structured reflection across the program, not in a single course; with an evidence ladder, falsification conditions and what the framework does not claim.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/problem-solving-deep-dive.html
- **The 3-Act Program Structure** — what the proposed Foundations & Agency, Integration & Mastery, and Specialty & Application structure is consistent with in the curriculum-design literature, what it is not grounded in, and the critiques it has to answer. It is a proposal, not institutional policy.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/three-act-deep-dive.html
- **Curriculum Measurement: Open Hypotheses and Research Agenda** — the forward, unvalidated hypotheses behind the measurement: how competencies might be compared with career demand, how skills fade and consolidate, and how to report uncertainty. Nothing in it is a validated finding.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/measurement-hypotheses-deep-dive.html
- **Graduate Outcome Criterion-Relevance Study** — the planned external check: do the competencies the tool emphasizes correspond to the occupations GC graduates actually enter? The pilot is complete and Phase 2 data are collected; the alignment analysis waits on a signed pre-registration and a second-coder check, with failure criteria committed in advance.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/graduate-outcome-validation.html
- **How the Competency Catalog Works** — how the shared competency list, course scoring, career requirements, alumni occupation mixes and the O*NET reference store fit together; what is live and what is planned. Section 9 covers the four business-acumen competencies.
  https://chiptoe-svg.github.io/gc-curriculum-tool/docs/competency-catalog-deep-dive.html

## Internal sources the papers cite

Some figures on these pages come from internal program sources that are not published. They are cited here so readers know where a figure originates, not so they can be opened:

- **GC Alumni outcome study** — validated first jobs of Clemson GC graduates, classified into the five career targets and joined to OpenAI's framework categories (data stamp 77cb46d1d20a5dd1, n = 661 validated graduates, 2016–26, first career job). Summary figures only; no individual records leave that study. Descriptive until the independent second-coder check, and the figures may be refreshed when the alumni roster matching settles.
- **GC Curriculum Tool live record** — intended and captured coverage, as read on 5 October 2026 for the main paper.
- **Creative-technologist career-path brief** — a program research brief compiling employer postings and pay surveys for creative-technologist, creative-operations and marketing-technology roles.

## How to cite

When quoting a figure, cite the paper or document and section, as the research pages do, for example "(AI and GC careers, §2)". Quote the documents, not these summary pages. Where a figure comes from a working paper, preprint, or vendor or trade source, say so. Statements the documents mark as "our reading" are the program's inferences, not findings.
