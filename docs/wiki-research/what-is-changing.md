# What AI is changing in GC careers

This page condenses sections 1 and 2 of the program paper *AI and GC Careers* (evidence as of October 2026): AI and early careers in general, then the four markets GC graduates enter. Back to the [[ai-careers-brief|AI and GC careers brief]].

## What the evidence says

### How "exposure" is measured

Most claims about "jobs AI will replace" start from an exposure score: how much of an occupation's task list a language model could speed up. Most descend from one 2023 exercise in which GPT-4 and human raters scored federal occupational task descriptions. They are useful for ranking screen-based, language- or image-heavy work, but weak at forecasting whether an occupation grows or shrinks, and because most studies share the same ratings, their agreement is less independent than their number suggests (AI and GC careers, §1).

Usage data from two AI companies (vendor research) show AI use crossing job boundaries. In ChatGPT work messages, about 44% of occupation-specific use concerns tasks that historically belonged to another occupation. In Anthropic's data, about 49% of jobs have seen at least a quarter of their tasks done with Claude. Design draws heavily on other occupations' tasks, and marketing tasks travel furthest into other roles; GC sits on both sides of that flow (AI and GC careers, §1).

### The entry rung is where the pressure shows

The least settled signal concerns new graduates (AI and GC careers, §1):

- **19%** relative employment gap for workers aged 22–25 in the two most AI-exposed fifths of occupations against the three least exposed, late 2022 to June 2026 (US payroll data; a working paper).
- **About 9%** lower junior employment at firms that adopted generative AI, within six quarters, through slower hiring (a working paper).
- **13% falling to 3–5%:** lower starting pay for graduates of the most AI-exposed tenth of majors after ChatGPT, relative to the least exposed, narrowing by year two (6.7 million Census graduate records; a working paper).
- **Texas:** a 10-point higher share of automatable tasks in a major went with a 1.7-point relative decline in graduates finding in-state work within a year, and first-year pay in more-exposed majors fell about 5% (2021–24). The authors conclude generative AI caused the declines (a central-bank analysis).

Other studies push back:

- Norway shows only a small, not statistically significant gap between the most and least exposed occupations (0.1% against 0.3% growth since October 2022).
- Unemployment risk in AI-exposed occupations began rising in early 2022, before ChatGPT, which fits the tech and interest-rate downturn as well as AI. In US job postings, too, the relative decline began before ChatGPT.
- One team finds the AI effect shrinks sharply, often to statistically indistinguishable from zero, once remote work is accounted for; another, using payroll data, finds it survives.
- US recent graduates' unemployment in summer 2026 was 7.3%, within the range since 2022. A New York Fed analysis estimates remote work can explain 64% of the rise in young graduates' unemployment.
- In Denmark, early-career employment in exposed occupations fell, but not at the firms that adopted AI chatbots. In Sweden, the ratio of 22–25-year-olds to older colleagues fell 3.9% more at AI-exposed employers.

Every one of these studies is a working paper, preprint, central-bank analysis or vendor research (AI and GC careers, §1). In the Dallas Fed's exposure measure, Graphic Communications (10.2%) sits with design, PR and communication majors (9.1–9.7%); that is exposure only, with no employment or pay results by major.

### Why it lands on the entry rung

When automation removes an occupation's less-expert tasks, the remaining job demands more expertise: wages rise, employment falls, and fewer people qualify to enter. That evidence covers earlier automation, so applying it to generative AI is an inference about mechanism. The payroll study offers suggestive, non-causal evidence that AI substitutes for codified knowledge and complements tacit knowledge built through practice. Two workplace studies suggest what this does to judgment; [[areas-of-focus]] covers them (AI and GC careers, §1).

### GC's four markets

- **Design and visual content.** Marketing budgets were about 7.7% of company revenue in 2025 and 7.8% in 2026, down from 9.5% in 2022 (Gartner surveys, read through trade press). Ad platforms now generate commodity variants directly. BLS projects graphic designers to decline 1.7% over 2025–35 and names AI tools as a reason. Real wages for web and digital interface designers rose 17.8% over 2015–25 while graphic designers' fell 1.2% (a vendor tabulation of BLS data). Census data show one of the largest relative earnings declines for Commercial Art and Graphic Design graduates in 2022–24, which cannot be separated from that period's advertising slowdown (AI and GC careers, §2).
- **Marketing.** BLS projects market research analysts and marketing specialists to grow 7% over 2025–35, about 82,000 openings a year; it is also among the most AI-exposed occupations. A trade association's 2026 report puts marketing jobs 27% below pre-pandemic levels, with senior and strategic roles holding and execution roles declining (AI and GC careers, §2).
- **Print and packaging.** BLS projects prepress jobs to fall 15.3% and desktop publishers 14.5% over 2025–35. Smithers, an analyst firm, projects digitally printed packaging and labels to grow from $22 billion in 2025 to $36.9 billion in 2030. One vendor's analysis of FDA data attributes 45% of 2024 US food recall events to labeling errors. In PMMI's 2025 survey, 95% of consumer-packaged-goods companies said they were struggling to hire skilled operators and technicians (AI and GC careers, §2).
- **Sales and client roles.** In a March 2024 survey of 81 commercial printers, 1.2% used AI for customer service and another 21.0% planned to within a year. No independent study of AI and print or packaging sales exists (AI and GC careers, §2–§3).

Across all four, the paper finds one pattern. **Shrinking:** resizes, ad variants and production art; routine reporting and first-draft copy; routine preflight, trapping and standard edits; estimating, order handling and file fixing; quotes, reorders and inside sales. **Growing:** brand systems and templates that AI varies, art direction and quality control of AI output; experiment design, measurement and marketing-technology operation; artwork-management systems, regulatory sign-off and versioning strategy; workflow automation and lean plant management; technical solution selling and key accounts (AI and GC careers, §2).

## Our reading

- **The entry rung is weaker; the cause is open.** On most of the 2026 evidence, remote work and the general hiring slowdown explain more of the weak entry rung so far than AI does. Two studies, from Texas and Sweden, find AI-linked declines, and none rules out a growing AI role (AI and GC careers, §1).
- **The shrinking work is where juniors learned.** If AI absorbs it, firms hire fewer juniors and expect new hires to start partly in the growing column. That makes it a curriculum question as well as a labor-market one (AI and GC careers, §2).
- **Growth in print and packaging is in versions,** in the paper's reading, but the analyst projection is not tied to shorter runs or more versions, and no source yet shows cheaper prepress causing more versions (AI and GC careers, §2; Demand elasticity, §5).
- **Treat every number here as provisional.** Most of the studies revise in place; a quarterly review tracks new research.
