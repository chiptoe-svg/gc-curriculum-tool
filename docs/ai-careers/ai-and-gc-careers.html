Graphic Communications · Clemson University

AI and GC careers

What the evidence says about AI and early careers, what it means for the markets Graphic Communications graduates enter, and how the GC curriculum fits now and needs to change.

For university, college and industry leaders Evidence as of October 2026 Status working draft Companion Demand Elasticity and AI Jobs

The short version

  1. AI is changing tasks faster than it is changing headcount. In US data, early-career hiring into AI-exposed work has weakened. How much of that AI caused is unresolved, and economy-wide employment shows no widespread displacement yet.
  2. The pressure falls on the entry rung. AI absorbs well-specified execution work, which is where new graduates have always learned. What remains asks for judgment that people used to build on the job.
  3. GC graduates work where AI is active. About 64% of recent GC first jobs are in occupations OpenAI's framework labels "grows with AI," against 12% nationally. That is a label, not a forecast. Their share in high-automation-risk occupations is close to the national rate.
  4. In each GC market the evidence points the same way, though it is thin for sales roles. Executing variants is being squeezed. Specifying, governing and integrating the systems that produce them is growing.
  5. The curriculum needs re-weighting more than replacing. Teach judgment as the scarce skill, require evidence that AI can't fake, and build the Creative Technology & Systems target, whose systems-and-workflow side the curriculum barely covers today.

1AI and careers, broadly

What is measured, and what is assumed

Most claims about "jobs AI will replace" start from an exposure score: a rating of how much of an occupation's task list a language model could speed up. Most exposure scores built for the language-model era, including those behind OpenAI's framework and the payroll and Census studies below, descend from one 2023 exercise in which GPT-4 and human raters scored O*NET task descriptions.1 Frameworks built on top of it add more model judgments. OpenAI's AI Jobs Transition Framework, for example, layers an AI-generated estimate of demand elasticity and net effect on that score.2 Its authors say its categories "are not job-loss forecasts."

Exposure scores are useful for ranking which jobs involve a lot of screen-based, language- or image-heavy work. They are weak at forecasting whether an occupation grows or shrinks. Because most studies use the same underlying ratings, their agreement is less independent than their number suggests.

AI use is task-level and crosses job boundaries

Two AI platforms that classify real usage against O*NET, the federal occupational database (Anthropic to its tasks, OpenAI to its detailed work activities) both find AI use spread widely across tasks. In ChatGPT work messages, about 44% of occupation-specific use concerns tasks that historically belonged to another occupation (16.8% of all work messages).3 In Anthropic's data, about 49% of jobs have seen at least a quarter of their tasks done with Claude.4 Design draws heavily on other occupations' tasks, and marketing tasks travel furthest into other roles.3 GC sits on both sides of that flow.

This matters for how careers are described. Usage shows how jobs change before job titles and federal occupation codes catch up. Emerging GC-adjacent roles, such as workflow and creative technologists, have no dedicated occupation code.

The entry rung is where the pressure shows

The most consequential and least settled signal is about new graduates.

Other studies push back. Norway's whole private-sector workforce shows only a small gap between the most and least exposed occupations through February 2026 (0.1% vs 0.3% growth since October 2022), which is not statistically significant.8 Unemployment risk in AI-exposed occupations began rising in early 2022, and graduates from 2021 on entered those jobs at lower rates, both before ChatGPT. That timing fits the tech and interest-rate downturn as well as AI.9 One team finds the AI effect shrinks sharply, often to statistically indistinguishable from zero, once remote work is accounted for; another, using payroll data, finds it survives the same control.105

Several of the newest studies find little sign of AI in young graduates' outcomes so far. In US survey data, recent graduates' unemployment in summer 2026 was 7.3%, within the range seen since 2022, and its gap with older graduates was stable at 4.5 percentage points; the authors note this does not rule out larger effects later.11 A New York Fed analysis estimates that remote work can explain 64% of the rise in young graduates' unemployment, and the rise predates the spread of AI.12 In Denmark, early-career employment in exposed occupations did fall, but not at the firms that adopted AI chatbots, and effects on earnings and hours above 2% are ruled out.13 In US job postings, the relative decline in AI-exposed occupations began before ChatGPT, and the authors find it "difficult to attribute the recent slowdown in entry-level hiring to AI alone."14 In Sweden, the ratio of 22–25-year-olds to older colleagues fell 3.9% more at AI-exposed employers; separations of the young rose (imprecisely estimated) and relative hiring showed no significant change.15 A vendor study of company spending finds heavy AI adopters grew entry-level headcount 12%, though they were already growing faster.16

One new study looks at majors directly. In Texas, after ChatGPT, a 10-point higher share of automatable tasks in a major went with a 1.7-percentage-point relative decline in graduates finding work in the state within a year; among those who found work, first-year pay for more-exposed majors fell about 5% from 2021 to 2024 relative to less-exposed majors. The authors conclude that generative AI caused declines for new graduates with automatable skill sets.17 Every study in these two paragraphs is a working paper, preprint, central-bank analysis or vendor research.

The same study scores each major's exposure. Graphic Communications sits with design, communication and PR, at about a third of computer science's exposure.17

The defensible reading. In US data, early-career hiring into AI-exposed work has weakened. How much of that AI caused is unresolved. Economy-wide there is no sign of widespread displacement. Among 22–25-year-olds, total employment slipped 1.9% as growth in less-exposed occupations offset most, but not all, of the decline.5 Our reading of the 2026 studies: on most of the evidence, remote work and the general hiring slowdown explain more of the weak entry rung so far than AI does. Two studies, from Texas and Sweden, do find AI-linked declines, and none rules out a growing AI role.

Why it lands on the entry rung

The mechanism is clearer than the size of the effect. When automation removes an occupation's less-expert tasks, the job that remains demands more expertise: wages rise, employment falls, and fewer people qualify to enter.18 Their evidence covers earlier automation, not generative AI, so applying it here is an inference about mechanism. The payroll study offers suggestive, non-causal evidence that AI substitutes for codified knowledge, the kind taught from textbooks and procedures, and complements tacit knowledge built through practice and mentorship.5

Two workplace studies suggest what this does to judgment. In a field experiment with 791 experienced professionals at Procter & Gamble, AI raised the quality of the solutions people produced, yet participants using AI were 9.2 percentage points less likely to expect their work to rank in the top 10%, a drop that came from those working alone with AI (the paper's Figure 10). What they made and what they judged it to be came apart.19 In a survey of 319 knowledge workers describing 936 of their own AI-assisted tasks, those with more confidence in the AI reported applying less critical thinking.20

Taken together: AI absorbs execution work, which was the traditional apprenticeship where judgment was learned after hiring. Employers increasingly expect new hires to arrive with some of that judgment already.

2AI in GC's field

Companion paper. For the economics of why cheaper output can mean more or fewer jobs in these markets, see Demand Elasticity and AI Jobs.

GC graduates enter four connected markets: design and visual content, marketing, print and packaging production, and the sales and client roles around them. In each, the evidence shows the same split between work that is shrinking and work that is growing.

Design and visual content

Marketing budgets averaged about 7.7% of company revenue in 2025 and 7.8% in 2026, down from 9.5% in 2022.21 In a vendor survey, most marketers expect the number of assets they need to multiply, driven by personalization and video.22 Under a flat budget, cheaper assets pay for more volume rather than more designers. Ad platforms now generate commodity variants directly, so much of that work never becomes paid design.23

The outcome data agree. BLS's 2025–35 projections turned graphic designers negative (−1.7%) and named AI tools as a reason.24 Real wages for web and digital interface designers rose 17.8% over 2015–25 while graphic designers' fell 1.2%.25 In Census data, graduates in Commercial Art and Graphic Design saw one of the largest relative earnings declines among creative and communications majors in 2022–24, even though the study's exposure index places that major mid-distribution (6th of 10 deciles), in its less-exposed comparison group.7 Those figures cannot separate AI from the advertising slowdown of the same years.

Marketing

Marketing is the GC destination where growth is best supported. BLS projects market research analysts and marketing specialists to grow 7% over 2025–35, with about 82,000 openings a year.24 It is also among the most AI-exposed occupations.524 In one survey roughly a third of marketers report cuts to entry-level hiring.26 A trade association's 2026 careers report puts marketing jobs 27% below pre-pandemic levels, with senior and strategic roles holding steady while execution-focused roles decline, and rates graphic design and copywriting among the skills most disrupted by AI.27 The work that grows is measurement, testing and running marketing technology. Routine reporting and first-draft copy shrink.

Print and packaging

Package demand follows physical goods and e-commerce, not the cost of artwork. AI savings in prepress therefore mean fewer hours per package, not more packages. BLS projects prepress jobs to fall 15.3% and desktop publishers 14.5% over 2025–35.24

The growth is in versions, in our reading. Smithers projects digitally printed packaging and labels to grow from $22 billion in 2025 to $36.9 billion in 2030, with corrugated the most significant opportunity; its release does not tie that growth to shorter runs or more SKUs. Regulatory artwork is a second growth area: one vendor's analysis of FDA data attributes 45% of 2024 US food recall events to labeling errors.28 In production, the industry frames AI as relief for a labor shortage: PMMI says packaging manufacturers "are recognizing that AI can help address some of their most pressing challenges, from workforce knowledge gaps to operational efficiency," and in its 2025 survey 95% of consumer-packaged-goods companies said they were struggling to hire skilled operators and technicians.29

Sales and client roles

Quoting, estimating, reorders and customer service are the exposed layer. In a March 2024 survey of 81 commercial printers, 1.2% used AI for customer service and another 21.0% planned to within a year (22.2% in total).30 Technical, consultative selling grows with complexity: substrates, sustainability rules, digital-versus-flexo economics, and transitions such as the 2027 move to 2D barcodes.

The common pattern

Shrinking: executing variants

  • Resizes, ad variants, production art
  • Routine reporting, first-draft copy
  • Routine preflight, trapping, standard edits
  • Estimating, order handling, file fixing
  • Quotes, reorders, inside sales

Growing: specifying, governing, integrating

  • Brand systems and templates that AI varies; art direction; quality control of AI output
  • Experiment design, measurement, marketing-technology operation
  • Artwork-management systems, regulatory sign-off, versioning strategy
  • Workflow automation, lean plant management
  • Technical solution selling, key accounts

The left column is where juniors have always learned. If AI absorbs it, firms hire fewer juniors and expect new hires to start partly in the right column.

The alumni data also show why federal codes undercount new roles. Thirty-four GC graduates have held 42 positions with workflow, automation or technologist in the title, and coders have scattered those jobs across seven unrelated occupation groups.31

1Production & Operations reshaping

Who
Makes it real: process control, quality, estimating, planning and scheduling, process engineering, equipment justification and managing people. At printers and converters, brand production and procurement, or agency production.
Outlook
Routine prepress (−15.3%) and press work (−8.7%) are projected to decline. The industry reports a shortage of skilled people, and packaging is forecast to be the growth segment.
AI's role
Vendors are building AI into equipment and workflow software (presetting, compliance checking; vendor claims), and printers report using it for estimating, scheduling and preflight. Much routine estimating and scheduling is expected to be automated; process engineering, equipment justification and managing people are not named AI uses.
GC graduates today
About 20% of first jobs, about half of them project management and line prepress, and nearly two-thirds at printers and packaging manufacturers. About a fifth of line-prepress starters later move into the systems or sales targets.31
Evidence
Strong on the decline of routine work; moderate on the shortage and AI uses (trade surveys and vendors).

2Sales Solutions & Account Management splitting

Who
The vendor-side solution seller: sales, account management, technical sales and customer service. The skills are technical and fiscal: understanding the process, building the ROI case, making the numbers work.
Outlook
Technical sales pays about twice the median and holds steady (BLS +1.2%); customer service, the on-ramp, is projected to shrink (−5.3%).
AI's role
The conversational and clerical layer is the most exposed: customer service ranks second of all occupations on one observed-exposure measure.33 Sales shows among the lowest estimated AI time savings (third-lowest of 22 occupation groups),34 which fits the technical and fiscal emphasis, though the savings are model estimates and BLS puts technical sales in its top AI-exposure category, "Very high".
GC graduates today
About 20% of first jobs, over half at printers and packaging companies. In pilot ACCGC data from nine GC programs, where Clemson supplies 268 of 603 records, 35 of the 38 sales records were Clemson's. About a quarter of customer-service starters later move into account or sales roles.31
Evidence
The thinnest of the five. No independent study of AI and print or packaging sales exists; this target needs its own research.

3Brand Strategy & Experience growing, squeezed entry

Who
The decision-maker at a brand or agency: directs creative work, channels, markets and technology in a business-driven strategy role.
Outlook
Marketing specialists and managers are projected to grow about 7% (2025–35), and pay roughly doubles from specialist to manager. Agencies are consolidating.
AI's role
Marketing is among the most AI-exposed occupations, and the signs of pressure point at entry roles such as report production. Strategy itself looks augmented rather than replaced, on limited evidence.
GC graduates today
About 20% of first jobs, three-quarters of them entry marketing roles. Strategy is a destination: 98 graduates later reached senior brand or marketing titles (matched on title words); more than half (55) started outside this target, most often in design or sales.31
Evidence
Moderate to strong on occupational growth; limited and indirect on the entry squeeze.

4Purposeful Design & Creative Generalist under pressure

Who
A designer with depth in one specialty (graphic, UX/UI or package design) who understands the business and technology consequences of their decisions, works fluidly across media, and builds AI tools for their own use.
Outlook
The most sobering evidence. Graphic designers are projected to decline (−1.7%) with AI named as a factor, design graduates' relative pay fell, and freelance design work shrank. Digital and interface design is projected to grow (+6.0%) and pays far more.
AI's role
Appears to substitute for per-asset execution. In one field experiment with experienced staff at one firm, it raised the quality of individuals' work, but those working alone with AI were less confident it would rank highly: what they made and how they judged it came apart. That suggests judgment is the skill to train.
GC graduates today
The largest target, about 28% of first jobs; graphic designers are the largest group, about half, and UX/UI/web is under 3% of all first jobs. No sign yet of a decline in GC graduates' graphic-design first jobs, though the numbers are too small to read as a trend. About a quarter of designers later broaden into art direction, UX/web or video.31
Evidence
Strong that graphic design is under pressure; the case for the cross-media designer who directs AI is reasoned, not yet measured.

5Creative Technology & Systems emerging

Who
The enabler: builds and runs the tools, systems and processes the other four targets use. Workflow, creative operations, asset management, colour management, IT support, systems analysis, platforms and integration. AI is central: implementing, benchmarking and evaluating it, not just using it.
Outlook
BLS projects strong growth for systems analysts (+7.9%), management analysts (+10.1%) and software developers (+10.2%), all rated very highly AI-exposed, though young developers' employment has fallen. IT support is projected to shrink (−3.5%), with automated tools such as chatbots named as a cause. It is better treated as a bridge than a destination.
Routes in
A direct route through early-career workflow, creative-operations, colour-management and automation roles, which the program intends to build; and a later move from production, operations or creative jobs, which is how most graduates reach the workflow side today.
Pay
Entry postings, which are few, about $52k–$90k; senior AI creative-technologist roles $120k–$226k after 4–10 years.35
GC graduates today
About 4% of first jobs, most of them IT support, analyst and platform roles; creative operations and colour management are 6 of the 26. Colour management is already a direct entry route; most workflow and creative-ops roles are reached later in a career.31
Evidence
Strongest qualitative case, weakest data. No dedicated federal occupation code exists, so these jobs are scattered across codes. Growth of the category is unproven.

4The curriculum today

The GC core is 16 courses across four levels. They run from orientation and digital graphics, through imaging, inks and substrates, to packaging, commercial printing, planning and control, and two seminars and two internships. A further 14 specialty courses, including the brand sequence, sit outside the program's career-target matrix today.

The GC curriculum tool records two different kinds of coverage, and they must not be merged. Intended coverage is what course catalog text claims. Captured coverage is what faculty interviews show students actually do, scored on a 0–5 depth scale. Captured evidence exists so far for only five courses. Intended counts include specialty courses whose catalog text claims a target, not only the 16 core courses.

What this shows. On paper the curriculum covers four of the five targets broadly. Target 5 is the clear gap in both sources, though it was scored under its earlier, AI-centred definition: one course claims hands-on depth (GC 3790, Brand Agency Leadership Practicum), and one course shows it in captured evidence (GC 4440, Current Developments & Trends). In GC 3460 (Ink & Substrates) students know and understand workflow concepts well but rarely practise them, a "theory without craft" pattern.

AI itself. AI appears in the learning objectives of one course (GC 3780, Brand Agency Practicum: "build skills with AI to ensure thoughtful and responsible inclusion of the technology") and in the projects or descriptions of two more (GC 1050 and GC 3790).

How far to trust this picture. It is partial. Five captured courses are not the curriculum, coverage scoring has not run since mid-June, twelve courses have no captured evidence yet, and the two internships have no recorded objectives. The direction is clear. The precise counts will change when scoring is rerun.36

5The curriculum ahead

GC's curriculum framework separates what students know, what they understand (can explain and judge), and what they can do, and requires evidence for each. The AI evidence does not call for a new framework. It changes the weights.

Three shifts in emphasis

  1. Treat judgment as the scarce skill. As AI makes production cheap, the differentiating capability is evaluating, selecting and directing work. A program that certifies polished output without the judgment behind it certifies the half of the job the market is de-pricing.
  2. Require evidence AI can't fake. A finished artifact no longer proves a student can do the work. Assessment should lean on reasoning through new cases, adapting to changed conditions, and critiquing and extending work.
  3. Describe careers from evidence, not from occupation codes. The roles GC graduates grow into cross federal categories. Destinations should be defined from postings, alumni outcomes and industry input.

What to build

  • Teach the content supply chain, not just content. Design systems, variable-data templates and scripting, generative pipelines, digital asset management and brand governance.
  • Lean into packaging versioning and compliance. Artwork-management platforms, regulatory labeling, 2D barcode standards, color across flexo and digital, and the cost of short digital runs.
  • Pair marketing with measurement. Testing and attribution are the work that grows when variants get cheap.
  • Build the missing first rung into the program. Internships, co-ops and capstones should leave students with evidence of systems and judgment work: a template system, an automated versioning workflow, a compliance-checked line extension.
  • Build the Creative Technology & Systems target. It is the highest priority and the least covered.

6Open questions

  • How does industry define Creative Technology & Systems roles? An industry panel would set the role's tasks, entry path and what counts as evidence of readiness.
  • What does AI do to print and packaging sales? No independent study exists yet. The Sales Solutions & Account Management target depends on it.
  • Is the entry-rung effect AI, the economy, or both? The studies disagree. The quarterly review of new research, next due by 9 December 2026, will track this.
  • Are GC's placement figures confirmed? The alumni job coding needs its independent second-coder check before any figure here is used as a confirmed finding.
