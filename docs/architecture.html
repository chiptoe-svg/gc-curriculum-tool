Architecture Overview

How the GC Curriculum Tool Works

A system-level view: the framework, the two sides of capture (what our courses develop and what careers demand), the agentic pipeline that produces the records, and every infrastructure piece that runs it — plus an honest account of how the framework matches the research it draws on.

Audience Technical reviewers, faculty curious about the AI, future maintainers Last updated 2026-10-19

§ 0The system today

A short orientation before the detail. Each point is covered in full in the section named.

  • One Mac, one database, campus only. A single Mac runs the Next.js app, Postgres, the vector store and the supporting services. The one way in is https://gcworkflow.clemson.edu:8443, reachable on the Clemson network; there is no off-campus path to the app (§9).
  • Personal access links. Faculty sign in with a personal link, issued from /admin/access and scoped to their own courses. The shared department login still works and is due to be switched off once every instructor has a link (§9).
  • CourseCapture. Course materials are read in the background at a depth the instructor chooses, scrubbed of student identifiers before they are stored, and indexed per course. A tool-using interviewer works from them; a synthesis pass drafts the Course Outcome Profile; the instructor reconciles and reviews it, and approval writes an immutable snapshot (§6).
  • A shared competency catalog. Competencies are catalog entries that any career target can claim. Each snapshot is scored once per competency, and the career views are rollups of those scores (§4, §11).
  • Career targets with occupation data. Each of the five career targets carries an occupation mix drawn from alumni first jobs, linked to an O*NET 31.0 reference store. Bundle cards, which place O*NET elements against catalog competencies, are under faculty review on /admin/bundles (§4).
  • Explore. A course-anchored chat that estimates the effect of a proposed course change and can save, compare and adopt the resulting scenarios (§5, §11).
  • The wiki and these documents. The wiki is regenerated from approved snapshots. Its public copy and the public documents are published to Clemson web space, which is reachable off campus (§9).
  • Built but switched off. The demand-minus-attainment sufficiency layer is built and its table exists, but the DEMAND_COVERAGE_SEAM flag is off and no employer demand has been rolled up (§4).

The engineering record of what is live, what is deferred and why is STATE.md.

§ 1Overview

The GC Curriculum Tool is a curriculum-analysis platform for Clemson's Graphic Communications department. It maintains a structured, evidence-backed record of what each captured course is evidenced to develop — scored across three learning dimensions at graduated depths — and aggregates those records into program-wide views that expose gaps, scaffold patterns, and prerequisite sufficiency at a granularity that committee intuition and periodic review reliably miss.

The system has four layers that work together: a framework (KUD+ with evidence discipline) that defines what counts as coverage and at what depth; two sides of capture that point the same framework at two different things — what our courses develop (attainment) and what careers require (demand); a set of surfaces (per-course capture and analysis tools, plus program-level views) that faculty and administrators interact with; and an infrastructure layer — a single Mac running the web server, database, vector store, document extractor, LLM providers, and a self-maintaining wiki. This document describes all four and maps how they connect.

For the stakeholder-facing picture of what the tool does and why it matters, see the executive brief. For the end-state vision and governance context, see the vision document. For the day-to-day faculty workflow, see the faculty guide. This document is the "system as a system" read — architecture, not aspiration.

§ 2The Driving Questions

Every design decision in the tool traces back to two questions, which the department has historically answered through committee discussion, gut intuition, and occasional formal review:

Q1 How well does the GC curriculum build students toward the careers we claim to prepare them for?
Q2 For any individual course, do the prerequisites students walk in with actually support what the course expects?

Q1 operates at program scale: it requires knowing what each course actually develops (not what its syllabus claims) and what the careers actually demand, then comparing the two. Q2 operates at course scale: it requires comparing a course's incoming-expectations profile against the confirmed output of its declared prerequisites. Both questions are unanswerable without structured, evidence-grounded records of what courses do — which is what the capture pipeline produces.

A distinction that runs through the whole architecture: Q1 has two halves. Breadth — "is this competency developed anywhere in the program?" — is computable today. Sufficiency — "is it developed deeply enough for what the career requires?" — depends on measuring career demand on the same depth scale and subtracting. The tool captures both sides of that subtraction now, and the differencing layer is built behind a feature flag (dormant, awaiting activation and the first employer data — §4).

The tool does not claim to answer these questions definitively. It produces AI-drafted, evidence-cited readings that faculty can review, dispute, and refine. The commitment is that those readings are disputable — backed by specific evidence, not syllabus aspiration — and that faculty disagreements are persistent rather than silently overwritten.

§ 3The KUD+ Framework

Every coverage judgment in the tool factors into three categories. The K/U/D planning vocabulary is drawn from Carol Ann Tomlinson's differentiated-instruction work (where "Know / Understand / Do" is a pre-unit planning aid) and is structurally compatible with Stage 1 of Wiggins and McTighe's Understanding by Design. The graduated depth extension and the cross-program mapping application are the framework's own contribution — not something either source prescribes (more on that honesty point below):

  • Know (K) — recall and identification of content: terms, facts, conventions, procedural sequences. Probe: "What is X?" / "Which of these is X?"
  • Understand (U) — reasoning about the why: principles, relationships, consequences. Probe: "Why does X work?" / "What follows from X?" / "When would you use X vs. Y?"
  • Do (D) — behavioral output: artifacts, performances, demonstrated skills. Probe: "Make X. Produce X. Demonstrate X."

Each dimension is scored on a 0–5 depth scale anchored to what the student does or grasps, not what the syllabus claims:

  • 0 — Not present (or listed only in aspirational syllabus language)
  • 1 — Exposure / restates the explanation as given / performs with per-step direction
  • 2 — Recognize / explains in own words / performs using a reference
  • 3 — Recall / predicts consequences / performs independently in familiar conditions
  • 4 — Uses correct terminology across the domain / reasons through novel cases / adapts to new conditions
  • 5 — Fluent across edge cases / critiques and extends / performs creatively with judgment, can guide others

These anchors matter because binary coverage ("covered/not covered") collapses in practice — most courses touch most relevant competencies at some level, and the matrix loses its ability to discriminate between a brief mention and an assessed performance. The depth scales restore that resolution. The authoritative rubric lives at lib/ai/prompts/shared/depth-scale.md and is injected into every scoring prompt; the schema (lib/ai/capture/schema.ts) enforces each depth as an integer 0–5.

Evidence discipline

Any score above the lowest exposure level requires evidence of student engagement, not just syllabus intent. Specifically: K=1 requires delivery evidence (a lecture, module item, or reading list entry); K>1, U>0, and D>0 require evidence of assessed student engagement, reasoning, or performance. This is the evidence-above-zero rule, and it is the load-bearing constraint that distinguishes a coverage map from a marketing document. It is not merely prompt guidance: the synthesis schema enforces it with three .refine() guards — a profile that scores above the threshold without a real evidence excerpt is rejected and retried. The model cannot emit a passing profile that inflates a score past its evidence.

Foundational competencies

Five program-wide competencies — Agency, Attention to Detail, Resilience, Curiosity, and Communication — are scored on D only. K and U are stored as null (not zero) for these rows, again schema-enforced. These are dispositions demonstrated through behavior; students don't recall facts about Resilience or reason their way through it — they exhibit it or they don't. Scoring K and U would imply the course tried and failed to develop the cognitive recall or reasoning aspect of a disposition, which is a category error. A foundational competency with D=0 is a valid and informative finding: the course doesn't create the conditions that develop the disposition.

Dissociation cases

The three-dimensional depth model surfaces patterns that binary coverage hides. K-high with U-low is jargon without rationale — students can name the thing but can't reason about why it works. U-high with D-low is theory without craft — students understand the principle but can't execute. D-high with U-low is craft without articulation — students can perform the procedure but can't explain why it works. The scoring prompt turns these into an active guard: a D≥3 with U≤1 must either downgrade or carry a rationale explicitly naming the dissociation, or downstream consumers treat it as undefended inflation. The depth model makes visible what binary coverage erases.

Why not Bloom's Taxonomy?

Bloom's revised six-level taxonomy (Anderson & Krathwohl, 2001) is the right tool for analyzing the cognitive demand of a specific assessment task. It is not well-suited to cross-program coverage mapping, for two reasons. First, its six levels produce a compound judgment across hundreds of course-by-competency cells that makes faculty review harder rather than easier — the distinction between Apply and Analyze is a calibration burden at scale. Second, Bloom is assessment-design vocabulary; it doesn't natively represent the behavioral output dimension (Do) as separable from the cognitive dimension, which the GC curriculum needs because craft skills in a professional field (pressing, color management, layout, prepress) involve behavioral performance distinct from cognitive understanding of the same content. So Bloom is used inside the interview conversation (the agent asks which Bloom verb best matches a high-stakes assignment) but no Bloom level is stored on the profile — KUD with depth is the stored mapping vocabulary.

How the implementation matches the literature

A fair question for any framework is whether the running software actually embodies the research it cites, or just borrows the names. For the GC tool, the research-grounded elements are operationalized at the strongest level — schema constraints and deterministic scoring code, not prompt suggestions:

K/U/D dissociations Independent measurement of three dimensions surfaces jargon-without-rationale, theory-without-craft, craft-without-articulation that binary coverage collapses. A "downgrade-or-name" consistency check in the scoring prompt makes the diagnostic an active guard; dimensional_patterns surfaces them to faculty.

What is stipulated, not derived — read this before over-claiming

The framework is scrupulous in its own background documents about where it invents rather than inherits, and the architecture preserves that honesty:

The 0–5 depth grain is a framework invention — a "working-hypothesis instrument." The cited traditions (Tomlinson, Wiggins & McTighe, AAC&U, EU Tuning) are consistent with a compact-and-leveled approach; none prescribes this scale.

KUD+ scores are course-opportunity scores, not validated student-attainment claims. They record what a course is designed to and assesses, with evidence — not a measured outcome for any cohort. The planned Graduate Outcome Criterion-Relevance Study checks a narrower question — whether what the tool measures is relevant to the first jobs GC graduates actually take — and does not turn these scores into attainment claims either.

The 3-Act program structure is explicitly a proposal, not institutional policy, and — importantly — the three named acts are not in the scoring engine. The scaffolding analysis implements generic introduction / practice / integration depth-phase sequencing by program order (the research-grounded part); the named acts live only as wiki/prose waypoints.

The five foundational competencies are a "defensible working set," not "the right set" — revisable as the corpus grows. And the artifact_verified evidence band is defined but currently unreachable in code (no student-artifact citation type exists yet), so credibility bands top out at "materials-supported" today.

§ 4Two Sides of One Ruler — Attainment & Demand

The single most clarifying idea in the whole tool: it is one ruler measuring two different things. We score every competency on one K/U/D depth scale (0–5). CourseCapture points that ruler at our courses and asks "how deeply can we prove students can actually do this?" Position Capture points the very same ruler at a job and asks "how deeply does a new hire need to do this on day one?" Because both sides speak the same units — and both are keyed to the same catalog sub_competency_id — the program's headline question becomes simple subtraction: demand minus attainment.

The attainment side — CourseCapture (supply)

CourseCapture measures what students are evidenced to attain, course by course (the full pipeline is §6). An AI interview agent works over the syllabus, Canvas materials, and an instructor interview; a synthesis pass emits one structured Course Outcome Profile that scores each competency on independent K/U/D depth against the shared rubric. Its defining trait is evidence discipline — the schema-enforced rule that no depth rises above its evidence (§3). Each confirmed profile becomes an immutable, versioned snapshot. A separate scoring pass then reads each snapshot against every catalog competency's own K/U/D descriptors, with no career target in the prompt, and writes one row per snapshot and competency to snapshot_competency_coverage — the attainment depths behind the /program heat map.

The demand side — Position Capture

Position Capture measures what a career requires of an entry-level hire, using the same depth scale. It runs a six-page partner flow, reached by a personal magic link (JD ingest → what makes the role unique → interview questions → trajectory → AI-rated "experiences worth having" → an agent interview), then a synthesis pass emits one immutable Position Profile in which each qualifying competency is scored required_for_success in K/U/D. Three disciplines keep the demand honest, mirroring the supply side:

  • A shared join key. Each qualifying competency and each rated experience carries a nullable structured sub_competency_id — the same FK the attainment side uses — populated best-effort by synthesis from the catalog list already in its context.
  • Day-one anchoring. required_for_success is anchored to what a new hire does on day one at entry level, not eventual mastery. Career trajectory (year 1, years 2–3) is captured separately and deliberately kept out of the scored demand, so a typical entry role lands near D3 rather than being inflated toward expert depth.
  • Demand-side evidence discipline. Just as a syllabus verb can't lift an attainment score, a vague endorsement can't lift a demand score: any above-floor depth must carry an evidenced_by note (what the partner actually said) plus a confidence level, or it's scored at the floor. "We want good communicators" is K1, not D3.

Many positions for one career roll up into a per-target aggregate (today a deterministic side-by-side summary; AI aggregation is deferred). Because the app is reachable only on the Clemson network, off-campus employers cannot currently open a partner link.

The shared competency catalog — what both sides are keyed to

The join key is a catalog, not a per-career list. sub_competencies holds every competency once, with its own K/U/D descriptors and an origin (migrated, gc_authored or onet_seeded). Which career targets claim which competencies lives in career_target_requirements — one row per target, competency, horizon (entry or growth) and weight source — so a competency can serve several targets, or none: an unlinked competency is a valid, active catalog entry. The views target_sub_competencies and target_coverage give the older per-target shapes to code that still reads them. When two competencies are merged, the retired id stays in the table, marked merged_into its survivor, and sub_competency_map maps every old id to its current one so historical references still resolve.

Two kinds of outside evidence now sit beside the catalog. career_target_occupations records each target's occupation mix per horizon — which occupations, by SOC code, GC alumni actually entered, imported from the department's alumni data (faculty can add rows of their own). The occupations in those mixes that O*NET rates are loaded into an O*NET 31.0 reference store (onet_release, onet_occupations, onet_element_ratings, onet_task_statements, onet_task_ratings). On top of that, bundle cards (bundle_cards, one per competency: what it includes and excludes, how it differs from its neighbors) and a review queue (bundle_members) place O*NET elements and task groups into each competency as core, related or fringe. The placements are AI-proposed (bundle-map-onet) and confirmed or rejected by a reviewer on /admin/bundles, which includes a blind golden sample and an agreement report so the AI's placements can be checked against independent human picks.

What this does not do yet: career_target_requirements has columns for a weight and a required K/U/D depth, but none are set from O*NET or alumni data. The rule is links before weights — the catalog-to-O*NET links are reviewed first, and only then would they inform how much each competency matters to a target. The full mechanics are in How the Competency Catalog Works.

The sufficiency seam — where the two sides meet

Because both sides emit K/U/D on the same rubric and both carry sub_competency_id, a career's required depth for a competency and the program's evidenced depth for that same competency become unit-for-unit comparable. The unified demand/coverage layer (the demand-seam "step 4" increment) is where they are actually differenced: it stores numeric per-dimension demand in a career_target_demand table — a partner-weighted average of position depths (the most-representative employers, by faculty-set weight, set the bar), against which the existing keyed course-attainment depths (ordinal MAX — the deepest any course reached) are subtracted. The result, per competency, is a signed gap — where a career needs a 4 and graduates evidence a 2, that gap of 2 is precisely where the curriculum falls short.

Built vs. not-yet-activated

Built and running: both capture engines. The CourseCapture pipeline, the shared catalog and per-competency scoring, and the /program coverage map on the attainment side; Position Capture (the six-page flow, immutable position records, the roll-up summary) on the demand side, with the join key, day-one anchoring, and demand-evidence discipline all enforced in the schema.

Built, switched off (the DEMAND_COVERAGE_SEAM flag): the sufficiency seam exists end-to-end — a pure engine (partner-weighted demand vs ordinal-MAX attainment, with an explicit no-data discipline: a sub-competency with no employer demand reads no_demand, and one that is demanded but has no coverage data reads no_coverage, never a fabricated gap), the career_target_demand table (it exists in the live database and is empty), a deterministic demand rollup, and a read-only sufficiency panel on the per-target page. It is unit-tested and inert until the flag is turned on. So Q1 sufficiency is computable, pending the decision to activate and the first employer data.

§ 5Surfaces

Every surface is served from one HTTPS address, https://gcworkflow.clemson.edu:8443, on the Clemson network. They fall into three groups: faculty surfaces, which need a signed-in session (a personal access link, or the department login while it remains on); public read-only surfaces, which need nothing; and the partner survey, which uses its own per-employer magic link. §9 explains how sign-in works.

CourseCapture
HTTPS · Faculty (signed in, scoped to the course) · /capture/<code>

Per-course interview that produces an evidence-backed Course Outcome Profile. Opens with a materials step — three source boxes (Syllabus / Canvas / Other materials), each showing provenance and readiness; a syllabus is required before the instructor can continue. A triage step then lets the instructor set how deeply each material is read before the interview starts. Reads Canvas exports, Google Drive content and uploaded files. A tool-using AI interviewer retrieves from the course's own vector index during the chat. For a bundled lecture/lab course, the Canvas box shows one import slot per code and both pages' materials land in the same index. The instructor reconciles and reviews the drafted profile; approval writes an immutable, versioned snapshot. A short how-to with screenshots is at /curriculum/howto.

Explore
HTTPS · Faculty (signed in) · /explore/<code>

A course-anchored thinking partner for proposed course changes. The agent grounds itself in the wiki, the cross-course material index and the course's neighbors (prerequisites and the courses that build on it), and its only source of numbers is an estimate_impact tool, which returns a scenario card: the change, the predicted K/U/D deltas, and a ripple ledger of effects upstream, downstream and on career targets. Predictions are hypotheses, not measurements. Scenarios can be saved, compared, and adopted — adopting writes the predicted depths into the course's capture draft as intended targets, a field kept separate from measured depths, together with the revised design.

Program Coverage Matrix
HTTPS · Faculty (signed in) · /program

Confirmed snapshots of the career-building courses (builds_to_career = true — GC Core plus the universally required major requirements), one row per course and instructor, crossed with each career target's competencies and rendered as a depth-aware heat map. Click any cell to see the evidence excerpt, confidence, and rationale, or to flag the reading for dispute. Scores come from the per-competency scoring pass (§4); refreshing stale scores and resolving flags are admin-only actions. Bundled lecture/lab courses appear as one row under a combined label (e.g. GC 3460/3461). The program-wide answer to Q1 breadth.

Scaffolding Strip
HTTPS · Faculty (signed in) · /program/scaffolding

Phase 1B view. Per-target heat map of depth, productive-failure conditions, and structured-reflection signals derived deterministically from confirmed snapshots. § 6The Agentic-Retrieval Pipeline

The pipeline that produces a Course Outcome Profile runs in three phases. It is built around retrieval rather than one large context window: when every material is visible to the AI at once, findings can emerge from pattern-matching across the entire context rather than from specific evidence, making provenance unverifiable. Retrieval keeps every finding the interview agent surfaces tied to a specific, citable source.

Phase A — Material Ingestion

When an instructor loads materials (Canvas import, file upload, Google Drive fetch, YouTube transcript), each material goes through an ingestion pipeline before the interview begins. The whole sequence runs off the request path in a background queue worker: an upload returns immediately and the page polls until the material is ready.

  1. Extract

    Raw content is converted to clean text. PDFs and Office files go through Docling (§9), which produces markdown with figure descriptions from a vision-language model; scanned PDFs are detected and sent through OCR. Legacy Office formats are converted via LibreOffice headless. Spreadsheets are compacted to strip empty rows, degenerate tables, and inline base64 image blobs that inflate token counts 20–30× over their information content.

  2. Privacy scrub + materials policy

    Two gates. First, a privacy scrub (lib/privacy/) runs inside the single function that stores extracted text, so nothing reaches the database, the digest, the embeddings or the vector store unscrubbed: it deterministically replaces Clemson CUIDs, Social Security numbers and email addresses with placeholders (emails are kept in syllabi, which are public documents) and records the counts on the material (course_materials.redactions). Names are deliberately not removed automatically — an AI name pass was tried and removed, because it also stripped inventors, historical figures and industry leaders. Second, a filename-based materials policy sets aside files that look like gradebook or roster data. A content-based risk detector still runs, but only to record a risk level for display; it no longer holds files back, since the scrub has already removed the identifiers. The same scrub runs on every wiki page before it is written, and generated profile text is redacted again before it renders on the public /view surface.

  3. Tiered routing + chunk / contextualize / embed

    Each material is routed to one of three ingestion depths based on its tier. Tiers are proposed at the triage step — deterministically for Canvas structure (the syllabus and graded work are high, slide decks middle) and by a light-tier classifier (material-classify) for other files — and the instructor can move any material to another level, which re-reads it at the new depth. High tier runs the full pipeline: a deterministic three-level hierarchical chunker (lib/capture/chunker.ts) splits the text into heading-aligned sections and detail chunks of ~500 tokens with ~100-token overlap; each detail chunk receives a short contextual-retrieval blurb prepended before embedding (the Anthropic contextual-retrieval pattern), so the vector encodes position as well as content; chunks and their parent sections are upserted into the per-course Weaviate tenant. Chunk IDs are deterministic UUIDs (SHA-256 of fileName|kind|position|sample), making re-ingestion idempotent. Middle tier produces per-unit summaries rather than raw chunks — slide decks receive per-slide vision notes via a vision model; prose materials receive per-section summaries — and these summary units are embedded at document level and cited at document granularity. Background tier generates a single whole-material digest embedded as one retrieval unit. Embeddings always use the campus Qwen endpoint (qwen3-embedding-4b, 2560-dim). The contextualize and digest calls prefer the campus gptoss-120b model with an automatic gpt-5.4-mini fallback (§9).

  4. Digest

    In parallel with chunking, each material receives a structured digest — a compressed but faithful summary optimized for the interview agent's at-rest view. Canvas Syllabus pages, assignments, and quiz content are kept verbatim (the agent needs exact assignment language and point values); long reference materials are digested. The digest substitutes for the full text in the interview context, with a per-row toggle for verbatim fallback.

After ingestion, an ingestion check-in agent makes one silent review pass over the assembled materials. It flags meaningful curation concerns — missing syllabus, stacked auto-set-asides, near-empty digests — but is silent the majority of the time.

Phase B — Interview Chat as Tool-Using Agent

The interview conversation is conducted by a tool-using AI agent, not a simple chat completion. On each turn, the agent:

  1. Loads the at-rest context

    System prompt + catalog values (description, prerequisites, learning objectives, major projects, required incoming skills) + per-material digests + a deterministic course-context brief of the linked courses (prerequisites from the Clemson catalog, and the captured profiles of those prerequisite courses) + a deterministic, verbatim session briefing (readiness, recent findings, the last instructor turn from prior sessions). This is the bounded context the agent operates from without retrieval.

  2. Decides whether to retrieve

    The agent determines whether the current finding or question requires specific evidence beyond what the digest layer provides. If so, it uses one or two tool calls (at most) before responding.

  3. Retrieves if needed

    Three retrieval tools: list_materials (see what's in the corpus), fetch_material_section (retrieve a specific section by chunk ID), and search_materials (hybrid vector + BM25 search across the per-course Weaviate tenant). Tool results carry chunk IDs that become citable sources.

  4. Responds in structured format

    Each turn returns a structured finding (the most consequential evidence-gap or conflict), a single focused question, citations (chunk IDs or instructor-turn message IDs), and a readiness signal (0–100, areas covered/remaining, a "good enough to generate" flag). When the instructor is done, one button asks for a last important question, then offers to generate the profile.

  5. Persists the turn

    Every message — instructor and agent — is appended to a session-scoped, append-only message log in Postgres, uniquely constrained by session ID and turn index, so there is no overwriting. The transcript permanently links to any snapshot produced from that session.

The interview conversation systematically covers seven areas: prerequisite sufficiency, objectives vs. evidence, cross-source conflicts, cognitive-level probe, threshold concept, foundational dispositions, and inaccessible content references. The agent weaves these in order of what the materials surface. Turns are streamed (NDJSON), so the faculty reviewer sees the response appearing in real time rather than waiting 3–7 seconds.

Voice input

The microphone button in the interview chat sends audio to an on-device Whisper transcription pipeline (§9). Voice is particularly useful for narrative explanations — describing what a capstone actually requires, or why a rubric criterion means what it does. Transcription replaces the in-progress text; the reviewer edits before sending. Mic works because the faculty surfaces are top-level HTTPS pages on the campus address, where the browser grants microphone access natively. Transcription stays on the Mac unless the operator explicitly enables the paid OpenAI fallback.

Phase C — Synthesis

When the instructor asks to generate the profile, a separate synthesis call — distinct from the chat — reads the full interview transcript plus the materials context and produces a structured Course Outcome Profile in a single pass: competencies (5–15 technical plus the 5 baseline foundational, each with K/U/D depth, evidence excerpts, rationale), incoming expectations, a verification summary, and interview notes. Every finding carries a source flag and citations (§7).

Reconcile and review

The draft then goes to the instructor in two steps. Reconcile: section by section (apparent outcomes, incoming expectations, outgoing competencies), the instructor says in their own words what is off, and the reconcile-feedback function proposes specific edits, which the instructor accepts, adjusts or rejects. Review: each competency appears as a plain-language card — what students know, understand and can do, in words rather than K/U/D codes — with "Looks right" and "Needs adjusting." Lowering an estimate takes effect immediately; raising one requires the instructor to name the evidence, which is frozen into the snapshot as a reviewer override with its reason.

Stress test

An adversarial stress-test reviewer runs automatically once after each profile is generated (and again on request). Its only job is to find problems: it checks citation-to-claim ratios, evidence excerpts against the claims they support, internal consistency between rationale and scores, source-flag honesty, and objective-misalignment claims against the transcript. Its concerns mark cards "Worth a look" in the review. It is advisory — its results are not saved with the profile or the snapshot. A thinking surface, not an authority.

Snapshot

When satisfied, the instructor approves and the system saves a snapshot: an immutable, versioned record freezing the profile JSON, the timestamp, the AI model that produced it, the instructor name, and a link to the transcript session. Snapshots are the artifact downstream views consume. Drafts remain mutable; only confirmed snapshots feed program-level analysis. Saving a snapshot starts three independent background tasks, none of which can block or fail the save: the wiki update (§9), a refresh of the course's slice of the cross-course material index (§9), and an objective assessment guide (objective-evidence-guide) that quotes each learning objective from the syllabus and names the Canvas assignments and rubric rows that measure it, with a deterministic check that drops any name not found in the course's own materials.

§ 7Provenance

Every finding in a Course Outcome Profile carries a source flag and citations. This is not optional decoration — it is a structural commitment the pipeline enforces at validation time.

Source flags

Three values:

  • materials — the finding is supported by citations that all trace to specific chunks in the vector store.
  • instructor — the citations all trace to instructor turns in the interview transcript (things the faculty member said that aren't in the materials).
  • inferred — no direct citations, or indirect ones. The weakest signal level.

The source flag is derived mechanically from citation types, not asserted by the synthesis agent. All chunk citations → materials; all transcript-turn citations → instructor; mixed or empty → inferred. This makes the flag verifiable rather than stylistic. In the review screens the three values appear in plain words — "From course materials," "From your interview," "AI's inference." A read-time evidence ladder layers on top, surfacing a credibility band (claimed / materials-supported / artifact-verified) so a high-but-instructor-claimed score is flagged, never gated.

Citations resolve to real sources

Every citation is either a chunk ID (resolving to a specific text segment in the course's Weaviate tenant) or a message ID (resolving to a specific instructor turn). The synthesis prompt and Zod schema both reject excerpt-only citations — a citation that exists only as a quoted string without a resolvable ID fails validation, causing the synthesis to be rejected and retried. This closes a class of hallucinated provenance where the model paraphrases "what it remembered" instead of pointing at a verifiable source. In the review UI, every source badge is a button: clicking it opens a citation drawer showing the actual chunk text or instructor turn, so faculty can verify directly that the evidence says what the finding claims.

The oldest profiles

Snapshots from the first, single-context pipeline and don't carry source flags or resolved citations. They render with a legacy banner and degrade the citation drawer to "excerpt only." They remain valid historical records and continue to feed program views; they just lack the provenance the retrieval pipeline produces.

§ 8Instructor and Time

Per-instructor capture

The same course code can be taught very differently by different faculty. One instructor's GC 3800 may develop Color Management at D=4 through a semester-long production project; another's may develop it at D=2 through lab exercises and a final exam. Both are valid findings — depth scores are a function of what students under that specific instructor actually do, not a platonic average. Capturing one and displaying it as the course's profile erases variance that may matter for advising and review.

The system supports per-instructor captures on the same code. Each session begins with an instructor identification step (a dropdown from a faculty roster); the name is stored with every session message and resulting snapshot. The public course listing shows "by [instructor]" attribution; the read-only profile view notes how many other captures exist when more than one is on file.

Snapshots are immutable

Once confirmed, a snapshot cannot be edited. It is a timestamped record of what the course was evidenced to develop at that moment, by that instructor, under that session's materials. The immutability is intentional: curricula erode over decades, and the only way to make that erosion visible is to preserve the dated records that accumulate. Faculty can re-capture as many times as they like; each new snapshot adds to the historical record. The Program Coverage Matrix automatically uses the newest non-retired snapshot per course.

Soft-retirement is available: a snapshot can be hidden from downstream views (superseded, or reflecting a version of the course that no longer exists). Retired snapshots remain in the database as history and can be viewed directly but don't feed program-level analysis. Hard deletion is not exposed.

Instructor variance as signal

When multiple instructors have captured the same code, the system surfaces the variance directly: the Program Coverage Matrix shows one row per (course, instructor) — each row the latest non-retired snapshot for that instructor, labeled "by [instructor]". Variance between instructors is visible side by side rather than hidden behind whichever capture happened last. Future phase work will add cross-snapshot diffs; per-snapshot attribution and the "N other captures on file" hint remain on the read-only views.

§ 9Infrastructure — The Pieces

The tool runs as a single Next.js application on one Mac Studio on the Clemson network. Postgres, the vector store and the document extractor run beside it; a GPU server on campus takes the heaviest extraction work. One codebase, one database, one host. This section walks every piece.

The web server and the one address

The application is Next.js 15 (App Router), run in production mode (next start off a prebuilt bundle) by launchd and bound to 127.0.0.1:3000, so it is not reachable directly from the network. Caddy (the system LaunchDaemon edu.clemson.gc.caddy, running as the unprivileged user _gcproxy from /usr/local/gc-caddy) terminates HTTPS at https://gcworkflow.clemson.edu:8443 with a Clemson InCommon certificate and proxies to the app. That is the only way in, and it is reachable only on the Clemson network. Caddy also holds the network-facing side of port 3000 and answers any plain-HTTP request with a pointer to the HTTPS address (a 426 error for /api paths), so nothing is served in cleartext. HTTPS matters for more than privacy: browsers allow microphone access only in a secure context, which is what lets voice input work on the interview page.

Sign-in and authorization

Middleware (middleware.ts, Node runtime because its checks query Postgres) sends every request through one gate (lib/auth/gate.ts). Public paths — /, /view/*, /wiki/*, the partner paths, and the two bearer-token machine endpoints (/api/mcp, /api/curriculum/search) — pass through. Everything else needs a grant, presented one of three ways:

  • A personal access link. An admin issues it on /admin/access (or with pnpm access:grant), naming the person, the courses it covers or "all courses," whether it may also add courses, and an optional expiry. Only a SHA-256 hash of the token is stored, in access_grants. Opening the link exchanges it for a signed gc_session cookie (HttpOnly, Secure, at most 30 days) and removes the key from the address bar.
  • The session cookie on later visits. Revoking the grant ends the session.
  • The shared department login (HTTP Basic Auth), which allows capture and course creation on every course but not admin. A DEPARTMENT_LOGIN switch turns it off everywhere; it stays on until every instructor has a personal link, after which a visitor without a session sees a "Sign in with your link" page.

Authorization is a pure, default-deny table (lib/auth/authorize.ts). Any live grant can read the faculty pages. Changing a course needs the capture capability with that course in scope. The admin surface — /admin/**, /api/admin/**, resolving flags, refreshing coverage scores and changing model settings — needs an all-courses grant with admin, which only a personal link can carry; the last such link cannot be revoked from the browser. Partner pages use a separate per-employer magic token checked against partners.magic_token, so partners need no account.

Database

Postgres 17 via Postgres.app, on 127.0.0.1:5433 (not the default 5432, to avoid colliding with any stock install), database gc_curriculum, launchd-managed (com.gc.postgres). It holds all application state — courses, materials, capture snapshots and messages, the competency catalog and career-target requirements, per-competency coverage scores, occupation mixes and the O*NET reference store, bundle cards and their review queue, Clemson catalog prerequisites, partner records and position captures, prerequisite edges, intended coverage, access grants, faculty flags, AI settings, and the rate-limit/cost tables. Course identity is stored structured — parsed prefix / integer course_number / number_suffix columns sit alongside the canonical code primary key (which stays the target of all 17 foreign keys), and a course_codes child table holds the paired lecture/lab codes of bundled courses (course_materials.source_code tags which page a material came from; null = the primary). The app connects via a node-postgres Pool (lib/db/client.ts); the schema is managed with Drizzle migrations, the larger catalog migrations are hand-written, each with a verify script and a rollback procedure.

AI providers + the provider abstraction

All model calls go through a provider abstraction at lib/ai/provider.ts — getProvider() returns one of OpenAI / Anthropic / Local / Campus / Fake, selected by the AI_PROVIDER env var. Switching providers is a one-line change with no application-layer edits:

  • OpenAI (current production, AI_PROVIDER=openai). The function-tier system maps light → gpt-5.4-mini and both default and heavy → gpt-6.1-sol at low reasoning effort (chosen after a blind evidence check of model outputs). Calls go through Clemson RCD's OpenAI proxy (OPENAI_BASE_URL) under the campus key rather than directly to OpenAI. Uses Vercel AI SDK v6 for tool-using + streaming completions — the tool-using agents on gpt-6.x models use the stateless Responses API (store: false) — and OpenAI strict JSON-schema mode for structured output (strict mode requires every property in required; every schema is written to that rule).
  • Anthropic (AI_PROVIDER=anthropic, claude-sonnet-4-6). Supports native PDF document blocks; a switchable alternative.
  • Campus (AI_PROVIDER=campus) — Clemson RCD's OpenAI-compatible cluster at llm.rcd.clemson.edu/v1. Default model qwen3.6-35b-a3b-fp8 (MoE, ~3B active params, ~180 tok/s); heavier glm-5.1-fp8 and deepseek-v4-pro reachable per-call. Free; cost reported as 0. Its provider implements a manual two-phase tool-call loop because the Qwen model doesn't reliably use the SDK's combined structured-output-plus-tools path.
  • Local (AI_PROVIDER=local) — omlx, a local OpenAI-compatible MLX inference server on localhost:8000/v1 running the Qwen3.6 family on the M4 Max GPU. Zero marginal cost, no data leaves the Mac. omlx lazy-loads models on first request and keeps them resident. (It is an external dependency, not managed by the project's launchd.)

Embeddings are decoupled from the generation provider. lib/ai/embeddings.ts always calls the campus endpoint's /embeddings with qwen3-embedding-4b (2560-dim), regardless of which AI_PROVIDER is active. A second pair of hot-path calls is also campus-first: chunk-contextualize and material-digest prefer the campus gptoss-120b model (thinking-off) and fall back to OpenAI gpt-5.4-mini on any error, independent of AI_PROVIDER (a per-function wrapper, lib/ai/analyze/chunk-llm-provider.ts; toggle with CHUNK_LLM_SKIP_CAMPUS). (The tier system stores OpenAI model names, so routing the other tiered functions through campus models would need a provider-aware tier refactor; that is deferred.)

Vector store

Weaviate v1.37.5, running as a Docker container (OrbStack, restart policy unless-stopped) with its data directory on the host, anonymous access, on 127.0.0.1:8090 HTTP + :50051 gRPC. Two multi-tenant Weaviate classes hold the retrieval index: MaterialChunk — detail-sized retrieval units (~500 tokens, with a short contextual-retrieval blurb prepended before embedding) — and MaterialSection — heading-aligned parent sections that carry no embedding of their own but provide the larger text window retrieved by "small-to-big" enrichment when a chunk hit surfaces. Both classes are per-course tenant-isolated, with each course occupying a tenant named coursecapture-<slug> (e.g. coursecapture-gc-4800). The interview agent's retrieval is therefore scoped to one course with no cross-course contamination. Retrieval uses hybridSearch — Weaviate's BM25 + vector blend — and performs parent-section ("small-to-big") enrichment: a detail-chunk hit pulls up its parent section's full text, so the agent reads a coherent chunk of document context rather than a clipped fragment. Point lookups by chunk ID (fetchChunkById) resolve the citation drawer in the review UI. The VectorStore interface (lib/capture/vector-store.ts) selects the Weaviate backend when VECTOR_STORE=weaviate, else an in-memory backend for dev/test; the retrieval ingestion path runs only when COURSECAPTURE_V2_INGESTION=1.

Curriculum-scale retrieval — the cross-course spine

Per-course tenant isolation is the right boundary for the interview agent (one course's evidence, no contamination), but it creates a silo problem for the curriculum-chat agent: /ask and the Explore "Ask" tab can reach the curated wiki prose and the structured KUD scores, but they have no path to the actual primary-source materials across courses. The cross-course spine closes that gap.

The spine is a single reserved program tenant — coursecapture-program (tenantForProgram()) — inside the same MaterialChunk Weaviate class, holding the UNION of all courses' detail chunks. It is a pure projection: no re-embedding, no new content. Each chunk is copied from the course's own tenant; the vectors are already there. Phase-1 scope is chunks only, no sections — so cross-course hits carry the chunk text + its contextual blurb, but there is no parent-section enrichment at program scope yet. That is an acceptable trade-off: the chunk + blurb is already a citable, sourced excerpt, and the per-course section text remains available via the per-course tenant for any follow-up drill-down.

Currency contract. A course's slice is (re)built from Postgres via listIndexableMaterialsForCourse, which filters to materials satisfying indexing_status = 'ready' ∧ ignored = false ∧ retired_at IS NULL. Anything the reviewer has retired, ignored (FERPA/policy/noise), or that was auto-set-aside drops out of the program tenant on the next refresh. Each copied chunk is stamped with provenance — the material's uploadedAt timestamp and the snapshotId that triggered the refresh — so a reader can trace a cross-course hit to its source material and the capture event that made it current.

Refresh lifecycle. The index is incremental: the same background hook that fires updateWikiForSnapshot on snapshot creation immediately follows with refreshProgramIndex(courseCode, { snapshotId }) (lib/capture/program-index.ts). It deletes the outgoing slice for that course (store.deleteByCourse(tenantForProgram(), courseCode)) and re-upserts from the current indexable set. A full rebuild (POST /api/admin/program-index/rebuild, which calls rebuildProgramIndex) is the one-time backfill path and the recovery path if the tenant drifts. Failures are logged and swallowed, matching the wiki-update discipline: neither refresh nor rebuild must ever block or fail a snapshot creation.

The tool: search_curriculum. Registered on the curriculum-chat agent and on the MCP server (lib/ai/wiki/curriculum-search-tool.ts), it runs hybridSearch over the program tenant and supports three modes: global (all courses, no filter); drill-down (pass courseCode to restrict to one course's slice); and per-course-diversified comparison (pass perCourse: true — the diversifyByCourse helper caps hits per course so a verbose course with many chunks cannot drown out a smaller course in a "how do treatments differ" comparison). Each hit returns courseCode + fileName + the chunk text + its contextBlurb + the chunkId for citation.

Why the spine feeds the agent rather than answering directly. External evidence backs this split. Xu et al. (2025), benchmarking curriculum-to-skill matching, found that pure embedding-similarity retrieval reaches only ~24% precision at identifying genuinely relevant skills, whereas retrieve-then-reason (RAG — retrieve candidates by embedding, then let an LLM judge them) reaches ~70–82%, a three-to-four-fold lift that holds across document types. The spine is the retrieval half; the curriculum-chat agent is the reasoning half. Querying the spine for answers directly would sit in the weak regime; routing its hits through the agent is the strong one (arXiv:2505.02324).

The curriculum-chat agent composes three retrieval layers: structured (typed-graph tools coverage_for_target / prereq_chain over the KUD coverage matrix and prerequisite edges), synthesis (the wiki prose via read_wiki / search_wiki), and primary-source (the spine via search_curriculum). The source-of-truth hierarchy is fixed: Postgres is authoritative; the wiki and the spine are both derived projections of it — the wiki is the curated narrative layer, the spine is the raw evidence layer. A future concept layer (Phase 2) would sit atop the spine, materializing cross-course "how each course treats this concept" cards into the wiki's concepts/ pages — not yet built.

Document extraction

Docling (IBM's document parser), run as the docling-serve HTTP microservice and selected by PDF_PARSER=docling. The primary instance (DOCLING_URL) runs on the department's GPU server on campus; a local instance on 127.0.0.1:5001 (launchd com.gc.docling-serve, CPU mode on Apple Silicon) is the automatic fallback when the primary fails (DOCLING_FALLBACK_URL). It converts PDF / DOCX / PPTX / XLSX / CSV / HTML / images to structured Markdown with real layout + table detection + OCR — vastly better than the in-process unpdf fallback, and it handles multi-format files unpdf cannot. An optional vision-language pass describes figures in slides and papers (DOCLING_VLM_*) — a real evidence source text-only extraction misses. Per-slide vision notes for slide decks go to a model gateway on the same GPU server through a local forwarder (launchd com.gc.dgx-forward), with a local model as fallback. Docling serves both the CourseCapture material pipeline and the Position Capture JD-ingest path.

Transcription

Audio transcription (faculty mic in the interview chat, and YouTube-video fallback) uses a three-tier ladder, first success wins: (1) omlx persistent MLX server — opportunistic, gated by a 100ms idle pre-flight, fast because the model stays resident; (2) the mlx_whisper CLI with whisper-large-v3-turbo on Apple Metal — free, on-device, ~1/60 realtime; (3) the OpenAI Whisper-1 API — last resort, used only when the operator has explicitly allowed it (WHISPER_BACKEND=openai, or WHISPER_OPENAI_FALLBACK=1 when the local binary is missing); otherwise transcription fails closed rather than send audio off the machine. For YouTube videos without captions, yt-dlp + ffmpeg downsample to 16 kHz mono before the same Whisper backends run; such transcripts are tagged (Whisper) in the filename.

Material storage

Uploaded course-material bytes live on the local filesystem under ~/.local/share/gc-curriculum-tool/materials/, served back through /api/storage/materials/<key> — a simple local path, sufficient for a single-Mac deploy. With the triage flow enabled (COURSECAPTURE_TRIAGE=1), approving a snapshot also deletes the course's raw uploaded files (raw_cleared); the extracted text, digests and vectors are kept.

The source of truth for every material is Postgres, in the course_materials table. Every downstream projection — Weaviate vectors, wiki prose, the cross-course spine — is derived from this row and must be treated as re-derivable. The row carries the extracted (privacy-scrubbed) text, the redaction counts, a SHA-256 digest for deduplication, an is_syllabus flag, and five lifecycle flags that control what downstream systems see:

  • indexing_status — pipeline state: 'pending' → 'queued' → 'indexing' → 'ready' (or 'failed' / 'skipped'). The background worker claims queued rows atomically with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED; only 'ready' rows feed the vector store and the spine.
  • tier — ingestion depth assigned at triage: 'high' (full pipeline: chunk → contextualize → embed → upsert), 'middle' (per-unit summaries embedded at document level), or 'background' (one whole-material digest as a single retrieval unit). Faculty-overridable.
  • ignored — exclude from all AI context: policy-blocked or noise. The curriculum-chat agent never sees ignored materials; neither does the spine.
  • auto_set_aside — set when the materials policy holds a file back (for example a gradebook-shaped spreadsheet), with the reason; kept as an audit trail even when the instructor overrides to ignored = false.
  • retired_at — curriculum-currency state: "the course no longer teaches this material." Distinct from ignored (which is about AI safety and noise, not curriculum change). A retired material drops out of the vector store and the spine on the next snapshot refresh; it remains in Postgres as history.

The curriculum wiki (a sibling repo)

A separate Git repository, gc-curriculum-wiki (cloned alongside the app at WIKI_REPO_PATH), is an LLM-maintained markdown knowledge base in the lineage of Karpathy's raw → compile → query LLM-wiki and Monteiro's "AI second brain" writing. The insight those sources press is that the compile stage — turning immutable raw material into structured, navigable, cross-linked pages — is the one almost every "second brain" skips, and skipping it leaves a query UI over an empty shelf (or a chat agent that confabulates to fill the void). This wiki exists to be that compile stage. It has two physical layers: a raw/ layer (immutable snapshot JSON + interview transcripts, ground truth) and a regeneratable narrative layer (courses/, competencies/, targets/, concepts/ pages + index.md). Being git-backed makes every regeneration an auditable, reversible diff, and gives the curriculum-chat agent a navigable, citable corpus without dumping the database into context.

This is running, not a plan: updateWikiForSnapshot (lib/ai/wiki/update.ts) generates prose with the heavy-tier wiki-update function, and writeAndPush (lib/wiki/git-ops.ts) — the only module that writes the repo — handles the git pull/write/commit/push, serialized through an in-process queue (concurrent regenerations would otherwise race on one working tree) with one pull-rebase retry on a remote race. It fires fire-and-forget via regenerateWikiInBackground at two moments: on snapshot creation (course + concept pages + index), and again after coverage scoring writes snapshot_competency_coverage — which is what lets competency and target pages exist at all, since those are derived from coverage rows that don't yet exist at creation time. Pages are emitted in bounded batches (WIKI_PAGES_PER_CALL = 6) because a full-coverage set can exceed thirty pages, whose combined output overruns the model's practical response size and silently stalls the call. A failure here is logged and swallowed — it must never break capture or scoring. When the whole wiki needs rebuilding, a one-pass refresh (scripts/wiki/refresh-all.ts) regenerates each course and program page once instead of once per capture.

Beside the generated pages, the wiki has a hand-written research/ section — question-led pages on AI and GC careers written for the curriculum committee. The generators never write there; it changes only through a separate sync step (scripts/wiki/sync-research.ts) that is scoped to that folder and gated on the lint. Every generated page passes through the privacy scrub before it is written.

The public copy. Because the app is campus-only, the public wiki lives elsewhere: scripts/wiki/snapshot-public.mjs crawls the app's public wiki pages into a static site published at tonkin.people.clemson.edu/pages/gc-wiki/, and a public visitor to /wiki is redirected there (lib/wiki/public-copy.ts); faculty keep the live pages. The public documents, including this one, are published the same way to tonkin.people.clemson.edu/pages/gc-curriculum/ from the committed main branch, so unpushed work never goes out. Both copies are refreshed by scripts/publish/clemson-refresh.sh — nightly at 05:00 (launchd com.gc.clemson-publish) and whenever a wiki write drops a trigger file — and a failed refresh posts an alert to the project dashboard. The older GitHub Pages addresses now redirect to these copies.

The determinism boundary (the load-bearing decision)

Every page is split into a deterministic skeleton and an LLM-written narrative, and the skeleton is deliberately the larger part. Deterministic, no model call: which pages a snapshot touches (computeAffectedPages resolves the fan-out straight from the coverage rows (the target_coverage view over snapshot_competency_coverage)), every K/U/D depth table, the "which courses build this / which competencies feed this target" lists, and the link targets. Model-generated: only the synthesis paragraphs and cross-course observations — the part that genuinely needs judgment. The reasoning: keep the deterministic seam wide so there is minimal new hallucination surface, and regeneration is structurally idempotent even though prose drifts slightly run-to-run (low temperature; accept prose drift, never structural drift). A security consequence falls out of this too — the model returns page paths as free strings, but the orchestrator writes a returned path only if it is in the deterministic requested set, dropping anything else with a hard log (an in-repo path guard against a steered or hallucinating model; update.ts, finding F8).

How cross-page links are made — and kept current

This is the part most surprising from the code, and the reason the wiki needs no link bookkeeping. Links are authored two ways by the model: inline [[wikilink]] / [[slug|label]] spans in the prose (the prompt instructs aggressive use — every competency, target, course, and concept mention becomes a link), and structured slug arrays in each page's YAML frontmatter (contributing_courses, develops_competencies, contributes_to_targets, …), which are recomputed from the Postgres substrate on every regeneration. Crucially, entity slugs are human-readable text primary keys — a course is gc-4800 (deterministic from its code), a sub-competency is brand-strategy, a target is brand-strategist — so the model can author a correct link without being told the target page exists.

Links are never stored as edges. They resolve late, at read time: resolveWikilinks (lib/wiki/markdown-helpers.ts) rewrites each [[slug]] by scanning the four type directories for a matching <slug>.md — found ⇒ a real link to /wiki/<type>/<slug>; not found ⇒ a visible broken-link code-span. Two behaviors follow, and both are intended:

  • Late binding / self-healing. A page may link [[scaffolding-analysis]] before that page exists; it renders broken, and the prompt treats a broken link as a signal that the next pass should create the page. The moment the target file lands in a later regeneration, every existing link to it heals — with no rewrite of the linking pages.
  • Bidirectionality is emergent, not maintained. A course page lists its competencies and each competency page lists its contributing courses, but there is no backlink index. Both sides are regenerated from the same coverage substrate, so they stay consistent by shared source rather than by a synchronized edge table — and can briefly drift if one side regenerates a pass before the other.

Why this shape, and what was rejected

Each of the load-bearing choices was made against a named alternative (full rationale in the 2026-06-04-wiki-update-compile-loop-design.md spec):

DecisionChosen approachAlternative consideredWhy the chosen approach won
Link model Late-bound text [[wikilinks]] resolved against the filesystem at read time A stored edge/graph table (FK rows linking pages) Slugs are deterministic text PKs, so links need no lookup to author and the corpus can be built incrementally and out of order; broken links self-heal and signal missing pages. An edge table would double-bookkeep relationships the prose already encodes and rot the instant table and prose disagree.
Retrieval Plain markdown navigation (the agent's read/list/search tools over files) A vector index over the wiki corpus The corpus is far below the ~100-article / 400K-word ceiling where markdown navigation stops scaling; a vector index here is overhead pretending to be sophistication. The split puts judgment where it helps and determinism everywhere else.
Compile-loop safeguards

The compile loop carries the safeguards its trust-hardening design called for (spec: 2026-06-11-wiki-trust-hardening-design.md): a deterministic lint (gc-wiki-lint / pnpm wiki:lint) flagging broken wikilinks, orphans, missing required sections, and under-sourced concept pages; a loud-not-silent reconcile that re-requests a requested page the model omitted from a batch and hard-logs if it's still missing; an input_hash watermark in page frontmatter for stale-detection; per-competency evidence-credibility markers (·claimed / ·materials / ·artifact, derived from each finding's source + citations) carried into the narrative on course and competency pages; prompt-injection fencing of snapshot-derived text; and a deterministic index (forced into the last batch with a manifest of every page in the run, so it can't lag pages produced earlier). Generated prose is also privacy-scrubbed for student identifiers on every write (§6). Still open: the ≥2-source concept-promotion gate (concept pages still generate on first qualifying signal — the lint flags under-sourced ones, but the compile doesn't yet withhold them), and a /refresh-wiki reconcile command (paralleling /refresh-state) that uses the watermark to detect and regenerate stale pages on demand. Tracked in STATE.md's Deferred/debt.

The curriculum as a queryable knowledge graph (wiki + typed-graph, over MCP)

The compiled wiki is the narrative query layer; alongside it sits a typed-graph query layer over the same evidence, and both are exposed to agents over a standard protocol. Two read-only structural tools — coverage_for_target (which captured courses build toward a career target, at what K/U/D depth, broken down by sub-competency, each with a confidence and an evidence excerpt) and prereq_chain (a course's direct + transitive prerequisites and the courses that require it) — answer relationship questions the prose flattens, computed directly from the per-competency coverage rows and the prerequisite map — the official Clemson catalog prerequisites first, the GC course sheet only for courses with no catalog entry, plus any confirmed prerequisite edges (lib/ai/wiki/graph-tools.ts; the pure shaping cores are split from the DB fetch so they're unit-testable without a database). They join the three narrative tools (read_wiki / list_wiki / search_wiki) and the spine's search_curriculum — six read-only tools in all — on two surfaces: the in-app /ask chat, and an MCP server at /api/mcp (Streamable HTTP, shared-bearer auth, fail-closed when the token is unset, campus-only like the rest of the app) that lets any Clemson-internal MCP-capable agent answer narrative and structural curriculum questions, grounded in the same evidence. A plain REST wrapper over search_curriculum, /api/curriculum/search, serves a sibling voice-interview app under its own bearer token.

The capstone move closes the loop: the CourseCapture interview agent itself consumes this layer. In full interview mode it is handed those four read-only program tools in addition to its course-local material retrieval — so while interviewing one course it can see the cross-instructor program picture (who covers a competency elsewhere and at what depth, the prerequisite chain into the course) and use it to probe handoffs or calibrate a depth claim. The discipline is fenced hard: program memory is reference, never evidence — the program covering something elsewhere never raises this course's K/U/D; this course's own materials and transcript remain the only basis for its scores. The net is a department's curriculum maintained as a live, evidence-grounded knowledge graph — narrative and structural — queryable by humans (/wiki, /ask), by external agents (MCP), and by its own capture loop.

§ 10Cost and Discipline

Function-tier model selection

Every AI operation is assigned to one of twenty-eight named function IDs (lib/ai/function-settings.ts), each with a default tier — light / default / heavy — that maps to a model. Any function can be overridden to a specific model and reasoning effort from /settings (an admin action); several of the busiest are set that way.

  • Light tier (gpt-5.4-mini) — per-material digest, per-chunk contextualization, material tier classification, ingestion check-in, materials analysis, prerequisite-gap decomposition, JD field extraction. These run often, so cost matters more than reasoning depth; digest and contextualization prefer the free campus model first (§9).
  • Default tier (gpt-6.1-sol, low effort) — the interview agent (capture-chat-agent) and the older single-context interview (capture-chat), profile synthesis (capture-scores), reconciliation, Explore (the agent and its impact estimates), curriculum chat, the objective assessment guide, prerequisite-edge seeding, intended-skills extraction, profile-field parsing (parse-profile-fields), course K/U/D extraction (extract-course-kud), target synthesis, bundle-card drafting, and the Position Capture functions (position-rated-items, position-interview-agent, position-synthesis).
  • Heavy tier (also gpt-6.1-sol today) — per-competency program coverage scoring, the adversarial stress-test reviewer, the wiki-update narrative generator, and the O*NET bundle placement (bundle-map-onet). Heavy-tier calls are rare, batched, or high-stakes enough to justify the cost.

Daily cost cap

Every AI call records its cost against a daily_cost table, compared with a daily cap (DAILY_COST_CAP_USD) shown on /settings with a 14-day sparkline. The guard has two modes (DAILY_COST_CAP_MODE): block, where calls past the cap return a 429 with an informative error, and warn, where they proceed and the server logs the overage. Production runs in warn so faculty work is never stopped mid-capture; the hourly com.gc.cost-cap-check job opens a GitHub issue when the day's spend passes the cap and closes it on a later day under the cap. The feedback-automation cron also checks the cap before each dispatch and halts at the limit.

"AI is your assistant, not your authority"

The cost model reflects the epistemic model: AI calls are tools for producing better-informed drafts that faculty review, not oracles that produce authoritative verdicts. Every depth score is editable. Every recommendation is a starting point. Every stress-test concern is advisory. The AI's job is to reduce the cognitive load of processing dozens of assignments and rubrics, surface patterns committee-level review tends to miss, and produce a disputable reading specific enough to argue against. Faculty deliberation is where the analysis becomes institutional record.

§ 11Connections

The Course Outcome Profile is not a terminal artifact — it feeds a network of connected views. Here is the explicit map:

Snapshots → Competency Coverage → Program Coverage Matrix

Each confirmed snapshot is scored once against the whole competency catalog: one AI call per snapshot reads its competency list and, for every catalog competency, decides whether and at what K/U/D depth its evidence supports that competency's own descriptors. No career target appears in the call, so a score means the same thing whichever targets claim the competency. The results land in snapshot_competency_coverage. Scores go stale when a snapshot or a competency descriptor changes, and an admin refreshes them from /program; each refresh also regenerates the affected wiki pages. The score is the depth at which the snapshot is evidenced to develop the catalog competency — not the snapshot's own internal competency score — because the snapshot organizes around what the course teaches and the catalog organizes around what career destinations require.

Career views are rollups of those per-competency rows through career_target_requirements. Every program-visible course counts toward a career rollup, while the matrix display shows one row per career-building course (builds_to_career = true) and instructor. Courses outside that set — gen-eds, free electives, and other catalog requirements that were never meant to build toward a specific career — are still captured and browsable, but do not get a matrix row.

Snapshots → Scaffolding Analysis

The Scaffolding Strip reads the same snapshots through a different lens: not "does the course develop this" but "at what depth, and with what evidence of productive struggle and structured reflection." Its deterministic engine classifies each cell as well-scaffolded, top-heavy, bottom-heavy, coverage-only, brittle-scaffold, or not-addressed. A brittle scaffold — a downstream course expecting mastery of a competency no upstream course introduces — is the diagnostic finding that drives the most redesign work.

Snapshots → Explore

Explore reads the focal course's snapshot together with its neighbors' — the courses it relies on and the courses that rely on it, with their competencies and incoming expectations — to reason about how a proposed change ripples up and down the curriculum. Its impact estimates compute those upstream, downstream and career effects explicitly, and an adopted scenario flows back into the course's capture draft as intended targets, never as measured depths.

Snapshots → Prerequisite Gap Analysis

Which courses are prerequisites of which comes from the official Clemson catalog: course_catalog_entries and course_catalog_prereqs hold the synced catalog (prerequisite, "before or alongside," and corequisite relations, with any-of groups), and that catalog-first prerequisite map feeds the interview's course-context brief, scoring context, and the prereq_chain tool. The skill-level gap analysis is a separate layer — persisted, skill-tagged prerequisite edges in a prerequisite_edges table: each row is a direct FK edge from a focal course to a prerequisite course, tagged to a specific sub-competency with expected K/U/D and a basis stamp (measured | intended | none). Edges are LLM-seeded from free-text prerequisites + incoming expectations and faculty-confirmed. computePrereqGaps differences the focal need against confirmed prerequisite attainment via ordinal-MAX aggregation (depths are levels, not quantities). Uncaptured prerequisites yield no_data, never an assumed gap. A companion intended-skills-extract function (migration 0031, the course_intended_coverage table) seeds syllabus-asserted K/U/D from catalog fields, banded claimed, to give a non-empty cold-start gap view before real captures exist — and these intended values never merge into measured attainment. Per-course gap view: /courses/[code]; program-wide graph viz is deferred. No edges had been confirmed as of October 2026, so the gap view stays sparse until faculty seed and confirm them. Structurally, this prerequisite graph with per-node depth is an evidence-gated knowledge space (the formal object behind adaptive-learning systems like ALEKS) — see background §2 for the framing and where the metaphor breaks.

Position Capture → Q1 Sufficiency (the demand seam)

The demand side (§4) connects here. Position Capture records employer-required KUD+ depth keyed to sub_competency_id; positions roll up into a per-target aggregate. The unified demand/coverage layer differences that demand (partner-weighted) against the matrix's evidenced attainment (ordinal-MAX) — turning Q1 from "is the competency covered?" (breadth, computable now) into "is it covered deeply enough?" (sufficiency). That layer is built end-to-end behind the DEMAND_COVERAGE_SEAM flag, which is off: the seam exists, it is just switched off.

Snapshots → Curriculum Wiki

The gc-curriculum-wiki sibling repo (§9) regenerates narrative markdown from confirmed snapshots. wiki-update fires fire-and-forget on snapshot creation, writing course + concept pages and the index; competency and target pages regenerate after coverage scoring. The curriculum-chat agent navigates this wiki via read/list/search tools and cites verbatim excerpts. Each write also triggers a refresh of the public copy on Clemson web space. The remaining compile-loop pieces (the concept-promotion gate and an on-demand stale-page reconcile) are listed in §9.

Industry Partner Input → Career-Target Descriptors

The partner survey feeds two pipelines with distinct jobs, and the distinction is the point — they are complementary, not redundant. Position Capture (the demand seam above) is demand-measurement: how deeply each sub-competency is required, scored in K/U/D and keyed to sub_competency_id. The synthesis pipeline (/admin/synthesis) is definition-refinement: it aggregates partner submissions into proposed updates to the career-target sub-competency descriptors themselves — what a competency is, not how deep it is required. These proposals are advisory — a faculty reviewer adopts, modifies, or rejects each before it takes effect. When a descriptor changes, existing coverage scores against that target are marked stale and queued for re-scoring; the program's self-understanding updates rather than silently inheriting a new descriptor with old scores.

The accumulation pattern

Most of the system's value is cumulative. A single snapshot is useful for the course it describes. Ten make the coverage matrix informative. Twenty give Explore's scenario estimates real neighbors to reason over upstream and downstream. A full cohort makes the scaffolding analysis diagnostic. And the demand side compounds the same way — more captured positions sharpen the sufficiency picture. The trial period is about confirming the per-snapshot analysis is credible before investing in the program-scale infrastructure that depends on accumulation.

§ 12Aspiration → Implementation

The companion documents — the KUD+ Background, the problem-solving and 3-Act deep-dives, and the vision — describe what the framework should do. This section traces each load-bearing claim to the code that implements it, and is deliberately candid about the most important distinction in the whole system: the difference between a rule the code structurally enforces and one it merely guides a model toward. Every row below was verified against the actual schema, prompt, or scoring code.

How to read the status

  • enforced — the code structurally guarantees it: a schema guard that rejects violations, a deterministic computation, a DB constraint, or a passing test. The model cannot emit a record that breaks it.
  • partial — implemented, but the guarantee is soft: enforced only by prompt instruction / model discretion, built on the happy path, or incomplete.
  • stipulated — a deliberate framework choice the docs themselves flag as invented (not derived), with no enforcing code by design.
  • unbuilt — aspired-to in the docs, not yet implemented anywhere.
The honest headline

Roughly a third of the aspiration is genuinely enforced in code. The course-capture schema guards (foundational null-K/U, the asymmetric evidence thresholds, depth bounds, the structured-post-mortem evidence gate) and the prerequisite-gap engine (ordinal-MAX, basis stamp, direct-edges-only) are real structural guarantees, reached by .parse() on the production path and pinned by tests. The remaining two-thirds splits into prompt-guided rules that depend on model discretion, stipulated framework choices invented by design, and unbuilt end-state aspirations. For the tool's headline question — does the curriculum build students toward the careers it claims? — both halves are built (course-attainment scoring and employer-demand capture), and so is the join between them: the demand−attainment sufficiency layer (engine, career_target_demand table, rollup, per-target panel; unit-tested). It is switched off behind the DEMAND_COVERAGE_SEAM flag and has no employer data yet. So Q1 sufficiency is computable in code, a decision and some partner interviews away from being visible.
