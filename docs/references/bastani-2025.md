# Bastani et al. (2025) — Generative AI Without Guardrails Can Harm Learning

**Full citation:** Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 122(26), e2422633122. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122

**Reference ID in background.html:** `ref-bastani-2025`; cited in §15 (research agenda: targeted AI practice for a measured prerequisite gap).

**Where it lives:**
- Local copy: `docs/references/_pdfs/bastani-2025-genai-without-guardrails.pdf` (8 pp.; pages "N of 8").

**Accessibility:** Full text held.

**What the background doc claims it says:** Field experiment, nearly 1,000 students at a high school in Turkey (p. 2): GPT Base (mimics ChatGPT) +48% and GPT Tutor (guardrails "designed based on teacher input", p. 2) +127% on practice; on a closed-book exam in the third part of the same 90-minute session, GPT Base students scored 17% lower than controls; the safeguards largely mitigated the harm (p. 1); the GPT Tutor arm showed no positive exam effect (p. 2). GPT Base was used as a "crutch" to copy solutions (p. 2); the exam measures short-term learning only (p. 2, footnote); students' self-reported perceptions were "overly optimistic" (p. 4). The page adds that the harmful condition was answer-giving help, so this is a caution about AI help with practice, not direct evidence about teachback.

**What it actually says (synthesis):** The held PDF is stamped "SEE CORRECTION FOR THIS ARTICLE" but does not contain the correction. Per the correction record itself (DOI 10.1073/pnas.2518204122, 20 Aug 2025; text via Europe PMC PMC12403119, read 2026-11-21), it corrects Osbert Bastani's affiliation (a production error); no finding changes. GPT Tutor's practice gain partly reflects that its prompt included the solution, letting students check answers (p. 4). Mechanism: students used GPT Base as a "crutch", copying solutions (pp. 2, 5). The authors note the exam measures short-term learning (p. 2, footnote).

**Verdict:** Consistent.
