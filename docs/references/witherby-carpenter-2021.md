# Witherby & Carpenter (2021) — The Rich-Get-Richer Effect

**Full citation:** Witherby, A. E., & Carpenter, S. K. (2021). The Rich-Get-Richer Effect: Prior Knowledge Predicts New Learning of Domain-Relevant Information. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition*, 48(4). DOI: 10.1037/xlm0000996.

**What the doc claims it says:** §2: "The 'rich-get-richer' effect, in which prior knowledge predicts new learning, was 'domain specific' in Witherby and Carpenter's (2021) three experiments: prior knowledge in one domain predicted new learning in that domain but not the other (abstract). The pattern was not perfectly clean — there was one cross-domain exception (truthful football items in Experiment 1), and in Experiment 2 cooking knowledge did not predict even cooking learning — so 'largely within-domain' is the fair summary."

**What it actually says (synthesis):**

Across three experiments, participants first completed a prior-knowledge test over two domains (football and cooking), then learned new, previously-unknown information from both domains (the to-be-learned facts were fabricated, so no participant could already know them, isolating acquisition from retrieval of pre-existing knowledge), made a judgment of learning, and were tested. **Headline finding (abstract, p. 1):** "the relationship between prior knowledge and new learning was positively related to new learning for items from that domain but not from the other domain" — i.e. domain-specific. This is the paper's own summary term throughout: "the relationship between prior knowledge and new learning was largely domain specific" (p. 6).

**The exceptions, which make "only within domain" an overstatement.** The pattern is not perfectly clean across all three experiments. The General Discussion (p. 10) states the qualified version directly: prior knowledge did not predict learning of new information "from the other domain... but not football information (except for truthful football items in Experiment 1)" — i.e., there is one cross-domain exception, confined to truthful football items in Experiment 1. Separately, in Experiment 2, cooking prior knowledge did not predict learning of new cooking information either — a within-domain null, not just an absence of cross-domain prediction. Of the paper's three experiments, only Experiment 2 is explicitly called a replication by the authors.

**Verdict:** Consistent with "largely domain-specific" / "largely within-domain" as the summary (the paper's own words, p. 6, plus its own qualification at p. 10). Not consistent with an unqualified "only within the same domain" / "does NOT spill across domain boundaries at all" framing — the paper itself reports one cross-domain exception (Experiment 1's truthful football items) and one within-domain null (Experiment 2's cooking-predicting-cooking). `measurement-hypotheses-deep-dive.html` §2 states both exceptions explicitly and uses "largely within-domain" as the summary term, which this card adopts as well.
