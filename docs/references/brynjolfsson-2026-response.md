**What the doc would claim:** Per the brief, that rerunning the identical specification on ADP payroll data, the AI gradient survives and grows while the remote-work gradient shrinks, with the difference attributed to data source (postings vs. payroll) and to measuring seniority by role vs. age.

**What it actually says (short verbatim quotes, with page refs):**

Framing of the disagreement: "Our findings contrast with estimates from Lambert and Schindler (2026), who find that accounting for work-from-home significantly attenuates the correlation between AI exposure and employment changes... In Online Appendix Section I... we follow the empirical specification in their work as closely as possible, finding that AI exposure continues to predict a decline in the junior share of new hires after accounting for work-from-home" (p. 27).

The replication result, stated numerically: "Estimated separately, both exposures enter negatively... The AI gradient is the larger of the two and grows steadily, from −0.48 in 2022 to −1.06 by 2025. When entering both jointly, the work-from-home gradient shrinks. The AI gradient grows, from −0.64 in 2022 to −1.52 by 2025. In the ADP analysis sample the joint variation therefore loads on AI exposure rather than on remote work, **the opposite of what Lambert and Schindler (2026) find in the Revelio data**, where the AI gradient is the one that does not survive joint entry. As the specifications are identical the different outcomes are driven by differences in the samples" (p. 132) — **CONFIRMED**, matches the brief almost exactly: AI gradient survives and grows (−0.64→−1.52), WFH gradient shrinks, under a stated-identical specification.

Attribution to data source: "This suggests that the results vary because of differences across the data sets, with Lambert and Schindler (2026) using data from online professional social media profiles and job postings" (p. 27) — **CONFIRMED** for the postings/profiles-vs-payroll attribution.

Attribution to role-vs-age seniority measurement: footnote 32 (p. 27): "One other difference between the studies is that the comparable estimate in Lambert and Schindler (2026) measures the junior hiring share outcome using a role-specific seniority variable coded by Revelio Labs. Our replication instead uses age to measure the junior hiring share. **This difference in outcome variable is another possible source of varying results**" — **CONFIRMED**, but with an important qualifier the brief's phrasing elides: the authors present data source and outcome-definition as two *co-equal, unranked* candidate explanations ("another possible source"), not a single resolved attribution to data source alone. They do not adjudicate between the two.

**Limits.** Working paper, not peer-reviewed, and explicitly self-described as descriptive rather than causal: "These analyses comparing AI and work-from-home are descriptive prediction exercises and not causal. Future work may seek to further separate the causal impacts of AI and work-from-home..." (p. 27). The reconciliation section is an appendix exercise (Appendix I), not elevated into the paper's six headlined facts. ADP is itself one payroll vendor's client base, not a population census — the same genre of external-validity caveat the paper applies to itself elsewhere (its own §5 compares ADP against CPS/ACS national benchmarks and finds the ADP pattern "more pronounced... than in national survey benchmarks").

**Why it earns a place here:** This is the direct, named rebuttal to Lambert & Schindler, run on an entirely different data source (payroll administrative records vs. résumé/job-postings) using as close to an identical specification as the authors could construct. It does not settle the dispute — it relocates it: the two papers now disagree on an apples-to-apples specification, which the authors attribute to sample/data-source differences and, separately but just as plausibly, to how each dataset codes "junior" (role-tag vs. age). For §16, that is a more precise and more honest framing than either side's standalone claim.

**Verdict:** CONFIRMED, with two corrections to the brief. (1) The source is not a short response note or blog post but §4.3/Appendix I of the already-archived Canaries working paper (Aug 2026 revision) — treat it as such rather than seeking a separate document. (2) The brief's "difference attributed to data source... and to measuring seniority by role vs. age" is accurate but should not be read as the authors picking data source as primary; they list both as unranked candidate explanations and explicitly decline to adjudicate.
