**What it says (verified against the full text; printed page numbers):**
- Knowledge is ascribed from behaviour: the "only objective mode of existence for this knowledge is behaviour over time"; quotes Ryle (1949, 57): "Overt intelligent performances are not clues to the workings of minds; they are those workings." (p. 66)
- Three meanings of "tacit" (§2.1, pp. 66–68): (a) acting intelligently without prior deliberation; (b) "we can know more than we can tell" (Polanyi) — the actor cannot articulate it; (c) even an observer cannot describe the action in rules. Quotes Schön (1983) on practitioners making "innumerable judgments of quality for which he cannot state adequate criteria" (p. 67).
- Behaviour-specific (mechanisable) vs regular acts (after Collins, 1995) — skilful work is mostly the latter (p. 68).
- Three types of tacit knowledge (§2.2, pp. 68–70): tacit knowing-how (skills, connoisseurship — "the right feel"); tacit knowing-that (background beliefs, frameworks); the tacit roots of all explicit knowledge.
- §3: acquisition mainly by learning-by-doing, example and criticism (pp. 70 ff.).

**Relevance:** supports the framework's choice to evidence Do from observed performance and graded artifacts rather than articulated rules, and the §7 caution that craft/connoisseurship competencies produce little quotable text. Cites Collins 1995 (not the 2010 taxonomy the page currently daggers).
