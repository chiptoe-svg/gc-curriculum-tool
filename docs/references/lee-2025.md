**What the background doc claims it says:** §16: "A related pattern appears in a survey of 319 knowledge workers reporting 936 examples of their own AI use: higher confidence in the AI was associated with less critical thinking (Lee et al., 2025). Both are evidence about judgment of AI-assisted work, not about selection skill directly, and the second is self-report. (The claim, sometimes attributed to these studies, that AI users produce less diverse outputs is earlier work Lee et al. cite as 'mechanised convergence', not one of their findings.)" The §17 entry: "Survey of 319 knowledge workers reporting 936 examples of their own AI use: higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking. Self-report."

**What it actually says (synthesis):**

*N and design.* "We survey 319 knowledge workers... Participants shared 936 first-hand examples of using GenAI in work tasks" (p. 1) — recruited via Prolific among people who self-report using GenAI at work weekly. Both numbers (319 people, 936 task examples) are correct and count different things. The design is entirely self-report and cross-sectional, measuring "enaction" of critical thinking (did the participant believe they performed it, yes/no, per task) rather than critical thinking itself, plus perceived effort per Bloom activity and three confidence measures (self, AI, evaluating-AI-output). The authors state plainly: "Our analysis does not establish causation" (p. 14).

*Headline finding.* Abstract: "a user's task-specific self-confidence and confidence in GenAI are predictive of whether critical thinking is enacted... Specifically, higher confidence in GenAI is associated with less critical thinking, while higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking" (p. 1). Quantitatively: confidence in the AI doing the task correlates negatively with enaction (β = −0.69, p < 0.001); self-confidence in doing the task (β = 0.26, p = 0.026) and in evaluating AI output (β = 0.31, p = 0.046) correlate positively. This is the exact pattern background.html attributes to it.

*"Less diverse outputs": prior work, not this paper's finding.* The paper raises "mechanised convergence" only as background motivation: "the tendency of AI-assisted knowledge workflows to be subject to 'mechanised convergence' [114], i.e., that users with access to GenAI tools produce a less diverse set of outcomes for the same task, compared to those without" (p. 2). Reference [114] is Sarkar (2023), CHIWORK '23 — a different paper. The authors immediately distance themselves from treating it as validated: "we lack direct empirical evidence for an interpretation that posits a connection between mechanised convergence and critical thinking. Output diversity is a proxy for critical thinking, and a flawed one" (p. 2). Output diversity is never measured in this study. This confirms background.html's parenthetical exactly: the "less diverse outputs" claim belongs to the cited prior work, not to Lee et al.'s own findings.

**Limits that bound how far this may be cited.** (1) Entirely self-report, cross-sectional; the authors explicitly disclaim causation. (2) "Enaction" is a subjective yes/no judgment about one's own critical-thinking behavior, not an observed or scored outcome — it cannot show objective skill or quality change. (3) Participants are already-employed, frequent (at least weekly) GenAI users recruited on Prolific — the sample is "biased towards younger, more technologically skilled participants" (p. 15) and says nothing about students or new graduates. (4) Six of seven authors are Microsoft Research staff; Microsoft is a GenAI vendor, a disclosure-worthy affiliation though the paper makes no product claims. (5) Occupation (including Arts/Design/Media, the second-largest group in the sample) enters only as a non-significant covariate — the study does not report design- or media-specific results.

**Why it earns a place here, for KUD+ and this tool:** together with Dell'Acqua et al., it is the second leg of §16's evidence that AI use changes the *evaluative* (Understand) side of work, not just the *productive* (Do) side — and it is careful to frame itself only as association, which is exactly the epistemic caution background.html's own phrasing ("evidence about judgment... not about selection skill directly, and the second is self-report") preserves. Its account of where critical thinking shifts under GenAI use — from task execution to "task stewardship," from problem-solving to AI-response integration — is a plausible description of the Understand-heavy skill the framework argues a program must now supply.

**Verdict:** Consistent. Background.html's §16 and §17 wording for this source is accurate on both counts: the N/example counts (319 workers, 936 examples) and the confidence/critical-thinking association are stated correctly, and the parenthetical correctly disclaims "less diverse outputs" as Lee et al.'s own finding rather than Sarkar (2023)'s, which this card's prior INDEX.md audit (2026-10-05) already identified and fixed. No further correction needed.
