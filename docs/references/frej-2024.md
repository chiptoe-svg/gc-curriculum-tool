# Frej, Dai, Montariol, Bosselut & Käser (2024) — Course Recommender Systems Need to Consider the Job Market

**Full citation:** Frej, J., Dai, A., Montariol, S., Bosselut, A., & Käser, T. (2024). Course Recommender Systems Need to Consider the Job Market. In *Proceedings of the 47th International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval (SIGIR '24)*, 11 pp. https://doi.org/10.1145/3626772.3657847 (arXiv:2404.10876v2).

**What it does (verified against the full text; page numbers are the PDF's):**
- **Course model:** each course is "two sets: 𝑐𝑟 the required skills with their proficiency levels, and 𝑐𝑝 the provided skills with their proficiency levels" (p. 5). Jobs carry required skills + levels (p. 5). Levels: beginner / intermediate / expert (+ unknown), mapped to ESCO (13,890 skills, filtered to 1,794 IT skills).
- **Learner state:** deterministic accumulation — "completing a course adds skills 𝑐𝑝 to 𝑢" (p. 6). **No forgetting.**
- **Job match (Appendix A.1, p. 9):** per required skill, partial credit min(have, need)/need (capped — exceeding a level earns nothing extra), **averaged with equal weight** over the job's skills; a job counts as applicable only if the average ≥ a threshold (0.8; p. 8). Equal weighting means a missing skill can be averaged away.
- **Goal:** "marketability" = number of jobs the learner can apply to; course sequences chosen by greedy / exhaustive / RL (DQN, PPO). Greedy is best for short sequences; PPO matches it and is faster for k = 4–5 (pp. 8–9).
- **Unknown levels (p. 7):** conservative defaults — unknown learner level → beginner, unknown job level → expert, unknown course level → intermediate; a course "requiring" a skill at or above the level it "provides" is corrected.
- **Validation is thin:** level extraction vs one expert on 212 skills from 10 job postings: "accuracy of 0.68" (p. 7); no user study; evaluated on 100 jobs × 100 courses × 52 résumés; the metric (applicable-job count) is computed by the system itself.
- **Self-stated limitation (p. 8):** "Assumption of Skill Acquisition" — completing a course is assumed to confer its skills; they call for a probabilistic acquisition model. (They do not discuss forgetting.)
- Motivation cites Deming & Noray (2020) on fast-changing job skills (ref. [8], p. 10).

**Relevance to this project:** closest published formulation of "career target as goal; skills accumulate course by course; readiness = match against job requirements with levels." Differences that are this project's contribution: evidence-anchored KUD depth (vs LLM-extracted levels from short descriptions), reinforcement/decay (they have none), Importance-weighted rather than equal-weighted requirements, program-level diagnosis (vs individual recommendation), GC domain (vs IT).
