# Kobayashi (2019) — Learning by Preparing-to-Teach and Teaching: A Meta-Analysis

**Full citation:** Kobayashi, K. (2019). Learning by preparing-to-teach and teaching: A meta-analysis. *Japanese Psychological Research*, 61(3), 192–203. https://doi.org/10.1111/jpr.12221

**Reference ID in background.html:** `ref-kobayashi-2019`; cited in §7 (Explaining to others as evidence of Understand).

**Where it lives:**
- Local copy: `docs/references/_pdfs/kobayashi-2019-preparing-to-teach-meta.pdf` (12 pp.; printed pp. 192–203).

**Accessibility:** Full text held.

**What the background doc claims it says:** Hedges' g = 0.35 for preparing-to-teach (28 comparisons) and 0.56 for teaching after preparing (16; Table 3, p. 199), both relative to studying without a teaching expectancy (p. 192); larger when the teaching is interactive (p. 192); interactive g = 0.84 (only 4 comparisons) vs non-interactive 0.48 (12) for teaching after preparing (pp. 198–199), a contrast the author warns may be confounded with other study features (p. 201); heterogeneity in the overall and subgroup analyses I² = 76–94%, delayed tests within one week (pp. 200–201).

**What it actually says (synthesis):** Outcome is the explainer's domain knowledge (surface and deep), against studying without a teaching expectation. Small study base; possible confounding of moderators with other design features; most samples aged 18+, and under-18 samples showed no preparing-to-teach effect (p. 201). Includes Fiorella & Mayer (2013, 2014) among its studies (not held; not cited directly). Says nothing about explanations as assessment evidence.

**Verdict:** Consistent.
