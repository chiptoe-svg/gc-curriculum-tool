**What this corrects, per the 2026-10-07 citation audit (findings AJ1, AJ5, AJ7, AJ8, AJ9, AJ10, AJ11):**

1. **Occupation count and coverage (AJ1).** An earlier draft stated "921 occupations, ~99.7% of US employment." Neither figure appears in the PDF (full-text search for "921" and "99.7" returns nothing); the report consistently rounds to **"900+ occupations"** and **"over 150 million jobs"** (p. 4) / "152M jobs" (p. 5, figure header). The corrected wording above ("more than 900 occupations covering over 150 million jobs") matches the report's own language. The data download itself has **923 O*NET-SOC rows** — a precise count, but of the *data file*, not a figure the report states about itself.
2. **The "foreword" (AJ10).** The PDF's own front matter labels and headers its opening piece "**Note from the Chief Economist**" (signed Ronnie Chatterji, OpenAI Chief Economist), not "foreword" — a functionally equivalent but not verbatim description. The corrected entry above uses "with a note from OpenAI's Chief Economist, R. Chatterji."
3. **The ~1.0 elasticity boundary rule (AJ8).** A reading that occupations near the elasticity boundary (~1.0) "should be read as unclassified rather than assigned" is not stated anywhere in the PDF; Fig. 2 (p. 12) labels its x-axis zones Inelastic / Unit elastic / Elastic with a dashed line at 1.0, and Appendix 3 (PDF pp. 28–29) calls elasticity "the least directly observed object in the current framework," to be read as "structured approximations... not as a substitute for" ground truth — but no explicit "read ~1.0 as unclassified" instruction exists. Any such reading is this project's own caveat, not the report's, and should be labeled as such.
4. **The report's chart and the data file disagree on at least one GC-relevant figure (AJ5/AJ9, and independently confirmed by the EU-extension card's own cross-check).** The report's Fig. 2 (US PDF p. 13) plots Graphic Designers at roughly **1.45** on its elasticity chart (a chart reading, not printed text); the separately released data download gives Graphic Designers an elasticity of **1.08**. Per the already-corrected §17 entry above, §16's per-occupation elasticity and employment-weighted archetype-share figures are cited to the **data download** (1.08), explicitly *not* the report's chart (≈1.45), with the discrepancy stated rather than silently resolved. The data-download-derived employment-weighted archetype shares used in §16 (11.9% / 17.7% / 23.2% / 47.3%) were independently recomputed from the data file and matched; Graphic Designers at 1.08 sits against a higher-risk-bucket maximum of 1.03, and Prepress Technicians at 0.56 sits against that bucket's median of 0.55 (AJ5/AJ9, reclassified supported after recomputation from the public data download).
5. **The headline framing (AJ7/AJ11) is correctly quoted:** "AI exposure alone is too blunt a measure to predict which jobs will face near-term disruption," with the four shares (18% / 24% / 12% / 46%) matching the report's own figures, and the authors' own disclaimer that these "are not job-loss forecasts" but a map — both confirmed verbatim against the PDF.

**Verdict:** `background.html`'s current §17 entries for both the report and the data download (quoted verbatim above) already reflect the 2026-10-07 audit's corrections — they state the right occupation-count language, correctly describe the Chief Economist's note (not "foreword"), and explicitly flag and resolve the report-vs-data elasticity discrepancy by citing the data download rather than the report's chart for any number used in §16. No further change needed to the live doc; this card exists to give those two reference-list entries a standalone audit record, since neither previously had one.
