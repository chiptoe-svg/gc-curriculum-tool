# Mollick et al. (2026) — Primer: An AI Framework for Instructor-Centered Educational Simulation Creation

**Full citation:** Mollick, E., Mollick, L., Przystanski, B., Ciccarelli, L. J., Bach, N., & Zarrow, L. (2026). *Primer: An AI framework for instructor-centered educational simulation creation* (SSRN Working Paper No. 7696720). Wharton Generative AI Labs, The Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania.

**Accessibility:** Full text held.

**What it is:** A design paper, not an evaluation. It describes an open-source, model-agnostic multi-agent system in which an Intake Agent interviews an educator (one question at a time, with uploaded materials, a private planning journal and targeted research), a Design Agent turns the interview into a design document the educator approves, and build agents produce a student-facing simulation in one of two templates: Roleplay (pursue a goal with a counterpart) or Teachback (explain a concept to a confused learner). The student experience runs mentor (preparation) → practice → feedback (reflection).

**Evidence status (read before citing):** The paper reports two-hour testing sessions with K–12 educators (February–June 2026, IRB-approved) that shaped the prototype, and states plainly that these sessions "do not establish its effectiveness as a learning intervention" (p. 11). Cite it as a described design approach (on background.html: sessions "informed its development but ... do not establish its effectiveness", p. 11, not "formative", which the paper uses on p. 12 for the field as a whole), never as evidence that the approach improves learning. Its learning-science claims rest on the works it cites (Roscoe & Chi 2007; Chase et al. 2009; Chi & Wylie 2014; Soderstrom & Bjork 2015; Star & Griesemer 1989; Bastani et al. 2025); cite those directly, from held copies, for any claim about learning.

**Ideas relevant to this project:** educator interview design (one question at a time, a pre-question check, a private planning journal, prior-knowledge questions with concrete subject examples, spoken progress cues instead of an unchecked checklist); Teachback as a way to elicit student-produced explanation (relevant to the Understand evidence rule); the "fair opportunity" concern that feedback may judge students on skills a simulation never gave them a chance to show (p. 13); the design document as a boundary object (pp. 7–8; not used on background.html, because the held Star & Griesemer 1989 PDF is a scan with no text layer, so a quote from it cannot be checked by check-citations).
