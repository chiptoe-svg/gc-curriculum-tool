**What the background doc claims it says:** §16 states: "A preregistered field experiment at Procter & Gamble (Dell'Acqua et al., 2026) found that AI raised the quality of the solutions professionals produced — the Do-side output — yet individuals working alone with AI were 9.2 percentage points less likely to expect their solution to rank in the top 10% (about 17% against 26% for individuals without AI): a gap between how good the work was and how good its makers judged it to be. Teams working with AI showed no such gap, expecting slightly more than the control group, so the miscalibration appears in solo AI use, not in AI use generally." The §17 reference-list entry adds: "AI raised solution quality and bridged functional expertise; individuals working alone with AI were 9.2 percentage points less likely than individuals without AI to expect a top-10% result, while teams with AI were not (Figure 10)."

**What it actually says (synthesis):**

*Design.* A pre-registered 2×2 field experiment at P&G (AEA RCT Registry AEARCTR-0013603), run May–July 2024 as a one-day virtual product-development workshop with 811 participants, 776 of whom were randomized across four arms: Individual No AI (control), Team No AI, Individual + AI, Team + AI. Teams were cross-functional pairs (one Commercial, one R&D professional). The AI tool was built on GPT-4/GPT-4o. Solutions (550 total) were rated 1–10 by a panel of 22 MBA/engineering evaluators (inter-rater agreement modest: ICC2 = 0.452, Kendall's τ = 0.153).

*Quality and the top decile.* AI raised average solution quality for both individuals (+0.373 SD, p<0.01) and teams (+0.392 SD, p<0.01); "Team + AI" did not significantly beat "Individual + AI" on average quality (p. 20: "their performance was not significantly different... AI's immediate impact appears to stem more from its capacity to bolster individual cognitive capabilities than from fundamentally transforming human-to-human collaboration"). The one place teams-with-AI pull ahead is the top decile: "teams with AI were 9.2 percentage points more likely to produce solutions in the top decile compared to the control mean of 5.8%... individuals with AI show a small positive effect, this effect is not statistically significant" (pp. 17–18).

*Calibration (the figure background.html cites).* "Expected Quality" is a binary self-report ("did you expect your solution to rank in the top 10%?") collected immediately after submission. The paper's prose generalizes across "AI-enabled participants" and "participants using AI" (p. 18: "AI-enabled participants were 9.2 percentage points less likely to expect their solutions to rank in the top 10% compared to the control group (p < 0.05)"), but **Figure 10 itself (PDF p. 40) disaggregates by all four arms**, and the drop is confined to one of them: Individual No AI ≈ 0.26 (dashed control line), Team No AI ≈ 0.28, **Individual + AI ≈ 0.17**, **Team + AI ≈ 0.29** (slightly above control). The reported "9.2 pp" drop (0.26 − 0.17 ≈ 0.09) is the Individual + AI arm's gap against control; Team + AI shows no gap and if anything a small positive one. There is no regression table for Expected Quality in Tables 1–10 — the finding exists only in prose and the figure.

*What is absent.* Full-text search finds no outcome measuring whether participants selected their own best/strongest idea ("selection accuracy" or similar is never defined or tested), and the sole occurrence of "internalize" (p. 21: "successful AI integration should focus on helping workers better recognize and internalize their improved performance capabilities") is advice about workers internalizing their own improved capability — unrelated to ideas being "less deeply internalized."

**Limits that bound how far this may be cited.** (1) One firm (P&G), one task (early-stage CPG product-ideation), one day, virtual, two-person cross-functional teams only — no same-function team arm, so nothing here speaks to cross-functionality per se. (2) Participants were experienced P&G professionals, not students or entry-level workers. (3) The calibration result is a figure-only binary self-report with no accompanying table, and its headline "9.2 pp" collides numerically (same figure, opposite sign, different arm) with Team + AI's actual top-decile gain — a real conflation hazard the authors' own prose does not flag. (4) This is the working-paper version; the peer-reviewed *Organization Science* text (and whether it retains the same wording or figures) has not been checked here. (5) Four co-authors are P&G employees and P&G funded the host institute, though the authors state full intellectual independence.

**Why it earns a place here, for KUD+ and this tool:** it is the strongest available field-experimental evidence for exactly the dissociation §5 names as a case the framework must surface — D-high/U-low, craft without the evaluative judgment to direct or critique it. AI raised Do-side output quality while, in the individual-alone condition specifically, degrading Understand-side self-calibration about that output. That the effect is confined to solo use (not AI use "in general") is itself the load-bearing nuance: it is evidence that *unmediated* individual AI use is where the judgment gap opens, which bears directly on whether a program should train students to evaluate AI-assisted work collaboratively or in isolation.

**Verdict:** Consistent, with a scope correction the current background.html text already carries correctly (as of the 2026-10-05 fix noted in INDEX.md). The §16 sentence must — and does — attribute the calibration gap to "individuals working alone with AI," not to AI use generally; it must not claim the paper shows AI users became worse at *selecting* their strongest idea, or that AI-developed ideas were "less deeply internalized" — neither claim is supported anywhere in the archived working paper (confirmed by full-text search). The §17 entry correctly flags that the published *Organization Science* version is unverified. One residual risk for future edits: the paper's own prose (p. 18) states the 9.2-pp calibration finding as if it applied to "AI-enabled participants" broadly; any future editor pulling a quote from that sentence rather than from Figure 10 would silently reintroduce the overgeneralization this card and the 2026-10-05 correction fixed.
