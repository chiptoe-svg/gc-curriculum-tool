## Open: older text in `background.html`, NOT changed (owner to decide)

Found while reading the same sources; all predate 2026-11-15.

1. **Substantive.** The sentence that generic transferable skills "are rejected by the same authorities §4 already cites (Willingham; Perkins & Salomon; Gick & Holyoak)". Perkins & Salomon (1992) write that "a monolithic local knowledge position is difficult to sustain" and that "the importance of local knowledge does not imply the unimportance of rather general knowledge that works together with local knowledge" (p. 5). They find spontaneous far transfer rare and argue it needs deliberate abstraction and bridging; they do not reject general skills. *Read from a scanned PDF by one reader; not re-read by the lead session.*
2. Bahrick (1984) reference entry: "733 people". The abstract says 733; the Method section says 773.
3. "Spaced practice, interleaving, retrieval testing, reduced feedback … all dramatically improve learning that lasts." Bjork uses "dramatically" for one spacing example only.
4. "Depth from massed, single-context drilling is … the fastest-decaying kind." Arthur et al. did not code massed versus distributed practice as a moderator.
5. Perkins & Salomon reference note: "far transfer requires explicit scaffolding". The source speaks of mindful abstraction and bridging, not scaffolding.
6. Frej et al.: "with no forgetting". The source says the assumption "overlooks the variability in individual learning outcomes" and does not mention forgetting.
7. Course-Skill Atlas: "roughly nine syllabi suffice for a stable major-level profile" is, in the source, an elbow criterion per institution-field-year record (8–10 for most fields); and the syllabi span 1966–2017, mostly after 2000, not "2000–2017".
8. Doignon & Falmagne: the "outer fringe … tells us exactly what the student is ready to learn" quote (also on the mapping page) drops the source's qualifier "assuming … faithful representation" (p. 16).
