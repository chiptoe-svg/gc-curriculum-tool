# Roscoe & Chi (2007) — Understanding Tutor Learning: Knowledge-Building and Knowledge-Telling

**Full citation:** Roscoe, R. D., & Chi, M. T. H. (2007). Understanding tutor learning: Knowledge-building and knowledge-telling in peer tutors' explanations and questions. *Review of Educational Research*, 77(4), 534–574. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654307309920

**Reference ID in background.html:** `ref-roscoe-chi-2007`; cited in §7 (Explaining to others as evidence of Understand).

**Accessibility:** Full text held.

**What the background doc claims it says:** Tutors' explanations may be reflective knowledge-building or knowledge-telling (manuscript p. 2); tutors tend to give knowledge-telling explanations across age, domains and formats, tutors trained to elaborate (new examples, principles, connections) scored higher on comprehension, transfer and delayed recall, yet even with training knowledge-telling often occurred more than knowledge-building (manuscript p. 19).

**What it actually says (synthesis):** A narrative review of peer-tutoring studies, not a meta-analysis. Knowledge-building = self-monitoring of comprehension, integrating new and prior knowledge, elaborating (abstract, p. 1); knowledge-telling = summarizing or delivering with little monitoring or elaboration (p. 2). Finds a pervasive knowledge-telling bias that training reduces but does not remove, and that returned when training cues were removed (p. 19). Notes knowledge-telling may be a necessary precursor and its total elimination "is probably not desirable" (p. 20). The mapping (knowledge-telling supports at most U1; knowledge-building begins at U2 with rationale in own words; consequences and novel cases U3+) is this framework's, not the authors'. The trained-tutor result is the review's synthesis of three studies (Fuchs et al., 1997; King et al., 1998; Ismail & Alexander, 2005), with one non-replication noted.

**Verdict:** Consistent. Quotes verified in the ledger.
