**What the background doc claims it says:** The original productive-failure paradigm. Students who struggle with ill-structured problems before instruction outperform direct-instruction controls on transfer.

**What it actually says (synthesis):** The 2008 paper in *Cognition and Instruction* provides the original "existence proof" for productive failure as a learning design paradigm. The study involved eleventh-grade science students (in Singapore) in a computer-supported collaborative learning environment working on Newtonian kinematics problems.

Two conditions, both collaborative problem-solving — **neither condition receives direct/explicit instruction**: in the productive-failure condition (PF/IS), students first worked collaboratively in small groups on ill-structured, complex problems — problems without a clearly specified solution method, requiring students to use their existing knowledge in new ways. In the comparison condition (WS), students first solved well-structured problems in small groups. Both groups then moved to individual well-structured problems, i.e. the design (Table 1, p. 386/PDF p. 8) is pretest → group work (IS or WS) → individual post-tests, with no instructional phase between the group work and the post-tests. The two conditions differ only in problem *structuredness*, not in whether instruction occurred.

Key findings: Groups in the PF (IS) condition struggled — they produced poor-quality solutions to the ill-structured problems; a group-level analysis found WS groups significantly outperformed IS groups on quality of solution during the group-work phase itself (p. 402). Yet despite this failure, these students significantly outperformed the WS-condition students on both the well-structured and ill-structured individual post-tests taken afterward — "these students outperformed their counterparts in the well-structured condition on individual near- and far-transfer measures subsequently, suggesting a latent productivity in what initially seemed to be failure" (abstract, p. 379).

**This paper's own mechanism is the contrasting-case / discernability argument (Marton, 2006), not "activation of prior knowledge" feeding a later instruction phase — there is no later instruction phase in this design (see correction below).** Kapur's own account, from the General Discussion: "the contrast between ill-structured problems followed by well-structured problems facilitated a spontaneous transfer of problem-solving skills, which, in the absence of the contrast, might have remained unrealized" (p. 410). Keeping the target content (Newtonian kinematics) constant while varying problem structuredness (ill- then well-structured, vs. well- then well-structured) is hypothesized to help IS-group students *discern* how to structure a problem — "the argument from discernability... keeping the target content the same and varying the level of problem structuredness may help students from the IS groups discern how to structure an ill-structured problem" (p. 411). Content analysis of the well-structured post-test solutions supports this: IS-group students produced better quantitative representations, assumptions, and contextual-parameter reasoning than WS-group students (p. 411) — elements Kapur argues were "learnt during the contrast provided by well-structured problems and not in the preceding collaborative problem-solving of ill-structured problems" (p. 411). Kapur is explicit that this is a "plausible but partial explanation" (p. 411), not a settled mechanism.

The paper identifies the two-phase generate-then-consolidate structure as the design unit **in later work (Kapur, 2016; Kapur & Bielaczyc, 2012)** — the 2008 paper itself, as corrected below, has no consolidation/instruction phase of its own; the "generate-then-consolidate" framing describes productive failure as subsequently theorized, not this specific 2008 design.

**Correction (citation audit, 2026-10-07; K82, substantive).** The original framing here and in `background.html`'s reference-list annotation — "students who struggle with ill-structured problems **before instruction** outperform **direct-instruction controls** on transfer" — overstates the design on two points: (1) there is no direct-instruction control condition anywhere in this paper; both conditions are collaborative problem-solving, differing only in problem structuredness; (2) there is no instructional phase at all, for either condition, between the group-work struggle and the individual post-tests — "before instruction" implies a later teaching phase that the design does not have. Table 1 (p. 386) confirms the three phases are Phase 1 (individual pre-test) → Phase 2 (group: 2 ill-structured or 2 well-structured problems) → Phase 3 (individual: well-structured post-test, then ill-structured post-test) — no instruction phase anywhere. The paper's own description of its two conditions is the one quoted above (Table 1). **Second, related correction (this pass):** the body text above previously described the comparison group as "DI-first students" who had been "scaffolded toward successful solutions" and invoked a "prior knowledge activation" / "affordance for subsequent instruction" mechanism that does not appear in this paper — that mechanism belongs to Kapur's later theoretical papers, not this one. The body text has been corrected to use "WS-condition students" and to state the paper's own mechanism (contrasting-case discernability, Marton 2006), quoted above with pages. `background.html`'s reference-list entry for this source should be checked against this correction if it still uses "direct-instruction control" language.

**Verdict:** Consistent on the core empirical result (the PF/ill-structured condition outperforms the WS/well-structured condition on individual post-test transfer measures). Not consistent, prior to this pass, on "direct-instruction controls" or "before instruction" (the 2008 design has neither), on describing the WS condition as having been "scaffolded," or on attributing a "prior-knowledge-activation / subsequent-instruction" mechanism to this paper — the paper's own stated mechanism is the contrasting-case discernability argument (Marton, 2006). All corrected above. The generate-then-consolidate two-phase framing is accurate as a description of productive failure generally, but belongs to Kapur's later papers (2016; Kapur & Bielaczyc, 2012), not to this 2008 design.
