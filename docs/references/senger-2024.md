# Senger, Zhang, van der Goot & Plank (2024) — Deep Learning-based Computational Job Market Analysis

**Full citation:** Senger, E., Zhang, M., van der Goot, R., & Plank, B. (2024). Deep Learning-based Computational Job Market Analysis: A Survey on Skill Extraction and Classification from Job Postings. arXiv:2402.05617 [cs.CL], 8 Feb 2024.

**What the doc claims it says (current, already corrected):** §11: "skill extraction and classification from job postings is an active field that its own survey calls 'emerging'; matching postings to ESCO, the EU's skills/occupations standard, is common in the classification work and, the survey notes, 'gaining popularity' (Senger et al., 2024)." The §17 entry matches this wording.

**What it actually says (synthesis, per the audit's verified quotes):**

The survey explicitly characterizes its own field as young, not established: "Because of its quick growth and its interdisciplinary nature, there is no exhaustive assessment of this emerging field" (abstract, PDF p. 1), reinforced in §3 ("crucial for establishing a common ground in this **emerging** field") and in the Conclusion, which frames ESCO-anchored extraction as a *rising* trend rather than a settled default: "Recent publications indicate two emerging trends in skill extraction. Firstly, extracting skills against skill bases like ESCO is **gaining popularity**..." (p. 9).

**Correction history (citation audit; SE1).** An earlier characterization of this source — "skill extraction... is a mature subfield, typically anchoring extracted spans to ESCO" — inverted both halves of what the paper says: the field is explicitly called "emerging," not mature, and ESCO-anchoring is described as "gaining popularity" (an emerging trend), not as the typical/default approach. `background.html` §11 has already been corrected to the "emerging... gaining popularity" wording quoted above, which matches the source.

**Verdict:** Consistent with the current (corrected) wording in `background.html`. The field should continue to be described as emerging rather than mature, and ESCO-anchoring as a rising trend rather than the typical practice, whenever this source is cited.
